# What a prefix mass coverage curve can establish

Let a true measurable partition at depth k have probabilities p_i>=0 summing to one. Sort descending, with S_m the sum of the largest m. N_delta=min{m:S_m>=1-delta}. Exchange of any selected smaller mass with an unselected larger one shows S_m is the maximum probability covered by any m cells. Thus N_delta is an oracle count for covering that much probability. It does not give an algorithm for discovering those cells without first computing their masses.

For a fixed recurrent gate history, the first k gates depend only on the first k inputs. A full-history event at depth T lies in its prefix-history cylinder. The union of M full histories projects into at most M prefix histories. Consequently its probability is at most S_M(k), for any consistent probability law and true partitions; independence of future coordinates is not required for the containment argument. Therefore N_delta(T)>=N_delta(k). This proves monotonicity, not multiplication by any observed growth ratio. Two positive children can have arbitrarily unequal probabilities, so a lower bound on total region growth alone does not give comparable growth of N_delta.

If a bounded correctness indicator is integrated exactly on covered probability 1-delta, and nothing at all is known on the remainder, that unknown contribution lies in [0,delta]. Coverage gives a sufficient worst-case interval of width delta (and midpoint error at most delta/2). Conversely, a particular estimator can handle omitted mass by known-correctness, analytic aggregation, symmetry, conditional expectations or error cancellation; it need not enumerate enough histories to cover 1-delta of all input probability. The oracle coverage count is not a universal lower bound for estimating accuracy. Numerical error on the covered contribution must also be bounded before using the interval.

The empirical array q_i consists of floating mass estimates. Sorting q_i/sum(q) computes coverage of that normalized discrete measure, not automatically of the true Gaussian partition. A total sum near one does not bound sum_i |q_i-p_i| or individual mass errors. For example, transferring epsilon between two positive cells keeps the total unchanged but changes a selected subset's mass. Directed individual bounds or a separate total-variation bound would be needed for a certified probability claim. If any q_i is negative or nonfinite, even the nonnegative discrete-measure interpretation needs repair; no silent clipping or renormalization will be introduced in the review.

Exact sums of stored binary64 masses can distinguish arithmetic/sort/threshold reporting from integration accuracy. The threshold itself should be labeled: decimal delta, binary64 delta and the stored rounded 1-delta need not be identical. A future saved checker must retain any boundary discrepancy, including whether the prior prefix mass is below threshold and the chosen prefix reaches it. It cannot certify geometry, original floating traversal, numerical mass integration, historical weights or future growth.
