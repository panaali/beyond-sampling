## Alternatives and discriminating tests
A. The nominal defensive sampler itself could be wrong: test its samples against independently computed order-statistic distributions and permutation invariants, not against its own numerical CDF.
B. Defensive coverage and a finite weight cap could be correct, yet empirical SE and the observed-weight gate could miss an unsampled mode: use an analytic two-mode target with exact probability and independently simulated multinomial counts. Contrast a proposal covering both modes with one covering only the first. This isolates missing modes from model-loading, truth noise and fitting errors.
C. Optional extension could change interval behaviour: separately test a low-hit control, with and without the literal source stopping rule.

Reliability measures: mean estimate/truth, empirical variance versus mean SE squared, |error|<=2SE frequency, gate frequency, unflagged noncoverage, extension frequency, and whether any >2x underestimate is silent. ## Pre-execution addition: conservative reporting alternative
At fixed n only, add a bounded-weight Chernoff/KL interval at delta=.05 for all analytic controls. Let b be the valid cap (5 for the missing/covered mixture controls, 1 for the identical-uniform low-hit control), x=p_hat/b, and include all means m in [0,1] with n*kl(x||m)<=log(2/delta), then scale by b and intersect with [0,1]. Numerical roots are bracketed on either side of x. Validity follows from exp(theta Y)<=1-Y+Y exp(theta) for 0<=Y=W/b<=1, independence, Markov's inequality and Chernoff optimisation; each excluded tail has probability <=delta/2. This is a finite-sample bound conditional on an independently fitted, then fixed proposal with a valid cap; it is not automatically valid after outcome-dependent stopping. Report coverage, median width/true p and whether it contains the omitted mass; do not present a wide interval as accurate estimation. This addition is frozen before any execution.
