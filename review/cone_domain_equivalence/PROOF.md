# Exact functional equality on a finitely generated cone

## Statement and scope

Let R=(r_1,...,r_m) be a nonempty finite list in Q^n (zero vectors and lineality allowed), C=pos(R), and S=span(R), of dimension d. Let

F(x)=Lx+sum_i v_i ReLU(b_i x)

be a homogeneous vector-valued function Q^n→Q^k interpreted over real x, with rational L, row forms b_i and column vectors v_i, k≥1. The difference of two such functions has this form by concatenating hinges and negating the second function's coefficients. No neural-history feasibility is assumed.

The finite procedure below returns zero exactly when F vanishes everywhere on C. It concerns exact represented cones and functions, not floating approximations, equality of labels alone, probabilities or runtime savings. Rationality permits exact algebra; the mathematical equivalence also holds for real coefficients with exact comparisons.

1. If d=0, return zero: C={0} and homogeneity gives F(0)=0.
2. Otherwise choose independent generators as the columns of B∈Q^(n×d). Every r_j has a unique coordinate rho_j with B rho_j=r_j. Put D=pos(rho_1,...,rho_m)⊂R^d. Substitute x=Bz, including L←LB and b_i←b_iB. The map B is injective; D spans R^d.
3. For each restricted row b, compute all b rho_j. If all are nonpositive, replace ReLU(bz) by zero on D. This includes the all-zero case. Otherwise, if all are nonnegative, replace it by bz and add its vector-weighted contribution to the linear part. If both signs occur, retain the hinge.
4. For each retained b, let a be its first nonzero coordinate and u=b/a. Normalize using
   ReLU(a u z)=|a|ReLU(u z)+1_(a<0) a u z.
   Add |a|v to the vector coefficient of u, and, when a<0, add v b to the linear matrix. Combine identical normalized u and discard exactly zero vector coefficients. Denote the resulting function

   G(z)=Az+sum_(u∈U) c_u ReLU(u z),

   where u are distinct normalized nonzero rows and each c_u≠0.
5. Return zero if and only if U is empty and A is the zero matrix.

The steps preserve F(Bz)=G(z) on D. The substantive claim is that a nonzero remaining canonical representation cannot nevertheless vanish on D.

## Lemma 1: full relative dimension and strict crossings

D is a full-dimensional convex cone. Because B was selected from independent generators, D includes the positive orthant generated by the corresponding coordinate basis; in particular int(D) is nonempty. More generally, the argument only needs span(D)=R^d.

For any w∈int(D), q∈D and epsilon>0, q+epsilon*w belongs to int(D). Indeed an open ball about epsilon*w contained in D remains contained in D after translation by q because D+D⊂D.

If a retained row u has both positive and negative generator values, choose such generators p,q. For sufficiently small positive epsilon, p+epsilon*w and q+epsilon*w lie in int(D) and retain their respective strict signs. The segment joining them lies in int(D), which is convex. Therefore H_u={z:u z=0} meets int(D). This crossing is inside the actual cone, not merely inside its span.

Consequently H_u∩int(D) is nonempty and open relative to H_u. Hyperplanes that only support/touch the cone never enter this argument: their generator signs are one-sided and were eliminated in step3. Rows vanishing on S are zero after restriction and were dropped.

## Lemma 2: isolate one retained hyperplane

If d≥2 and u,v are distinct normalized rows, H_u≠H_v. H_u∩H_v is a proper subspace of H_u. A finite union of proper subspaces cannot cover a nonempty relatively open subset of H_u. Hence there exists z0∈H_u∩int(D) lying on no other retained hyperplane.

If d=1, every normalized nonzero row is (1), so there is at most one retained hyperplane, {0}. Both signs force D=R; thus0 is an interior crossing and no avoidance argument is needed. The d=0 case was handled separately.

Choose a sufficiently small open ball around z0 contained in int(D) and disjoint from all other retained hyperplanes. On its two sides of H_u all other hinge signs are constant. The two Jacobian matrices of G differ by exactly c_u u, the outer product of the column c_u and row u.

## Theorem: necessity and sufficiency

Suppose F vanishes everywhere on C. Then G vanishes on D. For any retained u, both open sides of the ball from Lemma2 lie in D and G is identically zero there. Their Jacobians are therefore zero, so c_u u=0. Since u≠0, this forces the entire vector c_u=0, contradicting retention. Thus U must be empty.

Now G(z)=Az vanishes on the nonempty open set int(D). A linear map vanishing on an open set is zero, so A=0. This proves necessity. Conversely U empty and A=0 make G identically zero, and the preserving substitutions give F=0 on C. Thus step5 is an exact decision procedure for the stated function-equality problem.

The same conclusion follows if equality is given only on relint(C): under the injective coordinates this is int(D), and the proof already uses only this interior. Equality extends to the boundary by continuity and C=closure(relint(C)). Boundary and first-index ties are therefore preserved by exact margin equality; they are not dismissed as probability-zero exceptions.

If the test is nonzero, a point in relint(C) with unequal function values exists. This is not a lower bound on error probability, an actual feasible neural-history witness, or evidence of differing predictions. In lower ambient dimension C may have zero Gaussian measure in R^n; even on a full-dimensional cone different margins can yield identical winning labels.

## Two domains and reuse

Apply the procedure independently to C_a=pos(R_a) and C_b=pos(R_b), using each cone's own span basis. F_a=F_b on C_a∪C_b if and only if both tests return zero. This does not replace that union by its convex/conic hull, and does not prove the cost of a merged representation.

For network outputs, apply the theorem to the vector of differences from output0. Equality of that vector preserves every pairwise score difference and hence the first-index argmax, including ties. The theorem is stronger than label equivalence and does not characterize all label-preserving merges, common positive projective margins, approximate merges or longer-horizon nonlinear composition.

## Relation to the existing sufficient implementation

T11596's frozen primitive eliminates fixed-sign hinges but canonicalizes in the original ambient coordinates. A globally zero remainder there is sufficient; a nonzero ambient remainder remains unknown. The proof here justifies an extension only after exact restriction to span(C). On a full-dimensional cone the existing algebraic criterion is already necessary as well as sufficient, provided its exact implementation matches these steps. That mathematical observation does not upgrade unreviewed code, trained domains or saved results automatically.

No new algorithm has been implemented or run in this phase. A future implementation must independently verify basis/rank reconstruction and all substitutions, including zero-span and rank-one cases. Actual prefix ray archives still need separate coverage/history binding; treating their binary rays as exact rationals only defines represented cones.
