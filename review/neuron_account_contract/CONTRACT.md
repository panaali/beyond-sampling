# T244 interpretation contract for the owner-requested neuron account

This written prerequisite supports Claude's T241 numerical study. It does not change its ownership, targets or budget, and does not authorize any experiment. Sample size, streams, implementation, selection rules and execution limits still require the author's actual protocol. Sampling is appropriate for this interpretability study; success here is not the ARC deductive accuracy-estimator result.

## 1. Separate the questions

Encoding asks how well specified features predict activations. Intervention asks what a specified computational change does. Replacement fidelity asks whether a frozen substitute reproduces the original network on new inputs. Task accuracy asks whether predictions match second-argmax truth. Each can succeed while another fails.

Let Y be truth, F the original predicted class and G the modified class. Use first-index output argmax consistently, with explicit input-tie conventions for synthetic or finite inputs. The five disjoint outcomes are: both correct; original wrong and modified correct; original correct and modified wrong; both wrong with the same class; both wrong with different classes. They exhaust every evaluated row. Agreement is the first plus fourth groups; accuracy change is the second minus third groups, divided by the row count. Retain the underlying labels and denominators, not just percentages.

Pointwise, the correctness indicators differ only when F differs from G, hence

    |accuracy(G) - accuracy(F)| <= P(G != F).

Thus 99% agreement alone permits an accuracy difference as large as one percentage point. It does not establish T241's separate 0.1 percentage-point target. Conversely, equal accuracy can coexist with extensive disagreement. For agreement conditional on original errors, report the original-error denominator explicitly; a highly accurate task solver can erase the behavior it is intended to explain.

T201 independently demonstrated roughly 98% lookup task accuracy with roughly 95.3% agreement on the original example checkpoint. T224's fixed nonnegative projection improved hidden squared error and slightly improved agreement while worsening task accuracy on the reused panels. These are prior scoped observations, not fresh validation for T241. The exact same-summary counterexample excludes universal reconstruction from that particular summary but gives no useful population lower bound.

## 2. Define interventions as programs

Write the baseline as z_t=W h_(t-1)+w x_t, h_t=ReLU(z_t), logits=Wo h_T. State whether a patch replaces z_t before ReLU or h_t after ReLU, whether it occurs at one step or every subsequent step, and whether later states are recomputed from the patched state using the same remaining inputs. Patching h_T and recomputing only the readout is a terminal intervention, not a test of earlier recurrent use. Replaying original future states or gates after an intermediate patch is a different intervention and must be named separately.

Zero, mean and resample are three distinct operations. Freeze whether a mean is marginal, time-specific or conditional, which source rows estimate it, and whether all patched coordinates share a donor. Freeze donor population, conditioning variables, replacement-with/without-replacement, independent donor draws and reuse. Marginal resampling breaks correlations; joint vector resampling and condition-matched resampling preserve different ones. A donor's future information or target labels cannot silently enter a proposed online explanatory rule. No claim that donors are on the original conditional state manifold follows from coordinatewise matching.

The intervention's effect is a causal effect of the specified computational modification on the specified input/donor law. Off-distribution damage does not invalidate that well-defined effect, but it does limit inference about a neuron's unique natural semantic role. Large damage may reflect distribution shift or lost coordination; small damage may reflect redundancy or compensation. Mean-ablation results neither assign every neuron in a group the same role nor cover previously untested neurons and times.

Use paired original/modified outcomes on the same input. Preserve every intervention, including improvements, no effects, failures and missing cases. Freeze indexing conventions: the existing helper stores h_0 at index zero and input x_0 produces h_1. Do not conflate a neuron index with a time index or quietly omit the initial/final step. Null patching with the original value, patching an empty set and consistent neuron reparameterizations are useful later implementation controls, not executed controls in this document.

## 3. Encoding and gate-pattern conditioning

Keep the unit of splitting at the independent input sequence: all its times, activations and derived features stay in one partition. Fix training/selection/final-evaluation roles before results. All fitted means, centering/scales, feature dictionaries, regularization, gate-pattern choices, mode selection, thresholds and cumulative replacement orders belong to training or a separate selection split. A previously inspected holdout is reused evidence, not a fresh confirmatory set.

For an online account at time t, define maxima, ranks, ages and record flags using only x_0 through x_(t-1). Whole-sequence features are allowed as explicitly retrospective explanations, but do not establish an online recurrence mechanism. Missing second/third maxima at early times need declared indicators/conventions. A feature set that already determines the task label is not thereby sufficient for the network's deviations from that label.

Gate-conditioned regressions must state whether the gate pattern is obtained from the original network. Since a gate may be determined by the very preactivation being predicted, conditioning on its actual value is an oracle descriptive decomposition. It is not necessarily data leakage if openly specified and evaluated out of sample, but it is not a standalone cheap predictor unless gate inference and its errors/cost are included. Report pattern training/test occupancy, unseen-pattern handling and excluded mass/count. Do not report only successful frequent patterns.

Report per-neuron/per-time SSE, SST and sample counts as well as R²; specify the denominator convention and training-mean baseline if used. Test-set-centered SST is acceptable as an evaluation definition, not a fitting input. Zero SST requires an explicit undefined/exact-constant policy rather than an invented finite R². High aggregate or median R² need not preserve rare decision margins, small logits or individual outlier neurons. Hidden error and output-margin/label error are separate endpoints.

## 4. Mode coordinates and replacement execution

The inherited modes.py diagonalizes a 13-neuron restricted recurrent matrix for a chosen gate set, not the complete nonlinear RNN at every input. Its eigenvectors need not be orthogonal, real, unique or well conditioned. Freeze the matrix/gate restriction, eigenvalue ordering, normalization, phase/sign conventions, repeated-eigenvalue treatment and inverse definition. A coordinate patch uses the actual inverse and reconstruction basis, not an orthogonal projection unless orthogonality is established. Complex conjugate modes must be changed consistently so the reconstructed physical state is real. Numerical imaginary residual and conditioning errors need reporting; taking the real part is an operation, not an exact identity.

Specify handling of the three excluded coordinates and of inputs whose gates differ from the chosen set. Mode contributions cannot generally be squared and added as orthogonal explained-variance shares. For real residual coordinates a and state error V a, squared error is a^T V^T V a; cross terms vanish only under the relevant orthogonality. The complex version uses conjugate transpose. Clipping a reconstructed state to nonnegative values changes the intervention and must be reported separately. A complete state change can alter later gates, so restricted-matrix eigenmodes alone do not establish a global linear dynamical explanation.

A fitted replacement must disclose every original-network value it still consumes. Teacher-forced prediction of a state from the original past/gates differs from rolling the substitute forward using its own past. Evaluate them separately if both are studied. A recurrence replacement which continues to query original hidden states is an oracle-assisted substitute, not full reconstruction. Full input histories and externally computed order statistics may be legal but must be explicit and priced if an efficiency claim is later made.

Cumulative replacements are composed programs. Their effects can interact through future states, gates and the readout; they cannot be obtained by adding single-neuron accuracy changes. Freeze the order, grouping, stopping and fallback rule before the final test. Training-selected ordering is legitimate when tested untouched; selecting the best order on final outcomes consumes that test. Unchanged, single-component, group/mode and all-component arms retain distinct meanings. Extrapolation from a subset to all sixteen neurons is unsupported.

## 5. Paired uncertainty, case definitions and scope

For a fixed substitute and iid new input rows, label agreement is a Bernoulli outcome and an appropriate binomial interval can be reported conditional on the fitted model. Random donors require the corresponding independence/law to be justified. Repeated evaluations of one input are not independent new input evidence; shared donor banks or fits introduce conditioning and possible clustering. A conditional interval does not include fitting variability or generalize to other checkpoints automatically.

For accuracy change use the paired per-row difference, not two independent accuracy intervals or an unmatched truth estimate. One conservative construction is simultaneous binomial intervals [L_E,U_E] and [L_I,U_I] for erased and introduced errors; their difference lies in [L_E-U_I,U_E-L_I] by a union bound, without assuming the two events independent. Other justified paired methods are possible. To claim the population targets, distinguish point estimates from confidence statements: an agreement lower bound at least .99 and an accuracy-change interval contained within [-.001,.001] address the two respective targets. A nonsignificant accuracy difference does not establish equivalence. Confidence level and multiplicity policy remain to be frozen in the actual protocol.

There are many neuron/time/intervention/pattern/case comparisons. Either control a predeclared family of confirmatory claims or label unadjusted results exploratory; do not select favorable intervals after seeing outcomes. Reusing common inputs across arms improves pairing but does not create independent evidence across arms. Keep checkpoint-specific conclusions separate from a multi-seed population claim.

Define cases from the original input, not from the intervention outcome. The x9-max/second/below partition and the blind sign event overlap; cross-tabulate them or use an explicitly disjoint hierarchy instead of adding overlapping counts. Freeze gap definitions and any normalization, near-tie thresholds, handling of empty/tiny subsets and comparisons across training amounts or input laws. Diagnostic localization on a selected disagreement subset is descriptive unless its selection and later validation are addressed. More fragmented errors, conditional effects or correlations do not imply a random function or an architecture ceiling; T239/T242 address that distinct question.

Bind checkpoint bytes/architecture, numerical dtype/tie rule, original-versus-implementation parity, source revision, input/donor streams and exact row reuse. Preserve split membership, features/gate patterns, fitted coefficients and fit metadata, original/patched outputs, intervention masks/times, denominators, transitions, failed/empty cells and cost/runtime ledgers. Charge actual donor/feature generation, fit and original/modified forward work when reporting resources. These are requirements for a reviewable study, not a claim that unpublished work omitted them.
