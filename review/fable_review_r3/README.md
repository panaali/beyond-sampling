**Jargon.** FPE = one forward pass (5,948 FLOPs). pp = percentage point. RMS = root-mean-square error over a panel. Own-cost score F = (RMS / 0.85 pp)^2 x (C_eff / 1k FPE), with C_eff = max(cost, 1k); strict F <= 1, lenient F <= 10 (this is Directive 8 amendment 2 at DEV2's mean p(1-p) = .0725). Skeleton = positions and values of the top-k inputs; lows = the rest. Closure = a rule that replaces the lows by a point or a law. Floor = the error a closure would leave with perfect integration (measured by diagnostic sampling). Class = one ordered top-3 position triple (720 of them).

## A. What the evidence implies

### A1. The case that no cheap deductive estimator exists (heuristic bound in measured quantities)

Every deductive design is one of four shapes, and each now has a measured ceiling.

1. **Class-affine (frozen gates).** The most a forward pass can reveal is one exact affine map on one linear region (11 FPE with the Jacobian) or one verdict with its margin (1 FPE). Using one or a few maps per class leaves a bias of .89-1.79 pp with *perfect* integration (T274b, verified by T282), and the bias does not fall with more maps per class (exact last steps, quantile witnesses: T274b/T282), finer classes (T287) or more quadrature (T286, structural limit -2.0/-3.5 pp at Q=64). On DEV2 the frozen-gate oracle is -.87 mean / 1.34 RMS (T286). So F >= (1.34/.85)^2 = 2.5 even at zero cost, and >= 105 as implemented (7.9k-26k FPE). Flat in budget: this family is closed at every cost.
2. **Skeleton + closure.** The floor falls only by adding exact dimensions: R0 .51, top-4 .39, top-4 + spread .24 pp (T292, T296, T298; the last post hoc, T303 checking). The integral of the closure's 0/1 verdict needs n evaluations per class, and the measured error-vs-n curve is 2.75 pp at n ~ 1.5 (T297), 1.16-1.4 at n ~ 2-5, ~.32 at n ~ 36 (T302: total .57-.66 with the .51 floor). Three points give error ~ 2.75 n^-0.7 (weak fit). The strict bar needs error x sqrt(720 n) <= 26.9 pp sqrt(FPE), i.e. n >= ~270 per class (190k FPE, allowance .06 pp), where any floor >= .1 pp fails. At the 1k floor (n <= 1.4) the predicted RMS is ~2.2 pp, F ~ 7; measured: F 5.97 with cancellation and an inductive Newton step (T300), F 20.6 without (T302, 26k FPE). More exact dimensions multiply the classes (5,040 quadruples), so the floor and the cost cannot be cut together.
3. **Law propagation (mean field, cumulants).** With *exact* class moments a Gaussian readout is -11 pp off (T286 oracle); per blind unit the exact Gaussian orthant passes 36% and a Gaussian state through step 7 with exact last 3 steps passes 30% (T295). The margin law is a mixture with hard edges made by gates switching inside the class; the components needed are the decision-relevant branches, growing 5-10x per step (N99 47/698/3,518 over the last 1-3 steps). So this family either merges (and loses the edges) or branches (and becomes family 4).
4. **Bounds and exact enumeration.** Width/margin grows ~50x per step; 0/180 sectors decided at T=10 (T289, verified by T279). Exact regions grow 15-30x per step (H-E13); the rigorous bound is >= 1.35M histories, the extrapolation 1e10-1e13, each 10-D cone mass ~1e6 FLOPs.

**The common root** [inferred]: training puts the pieces that decide accuracy at the tie walls, 37-44x finer than far from ties (H-E15, confirmed), and a weak retrain's extra error is a diffuse widening of the soft tail over ~44 triples carrying 60% of the cross-model variance (ANAT-80). To hit .85 pp RMS at 1k FPE an estimator must explain 93% of a 3.2 pp spread with 1.4 evaluations per class, i.e. resolve ~44 model-specific per-class tail errors of ~.03-.06 pp each from one point and one tangent per class. The measured one-point designs (TB, R0) misplace ~6% of the skeleton mass per class in every region, including the bulk (T297). The per-step amplification factors (x5-50) say that any attempt to *infer* the rest of a class from its one point, by bound or by branch, costs the full depth.

**Bottom line of A1.** F_min across all known families, with each family's *best measured* floor and the measured error-vs-cost curve, is ~5-7 with cancellation, ~20 without, and >= 2.5 even for a free class-affine map. Strict (F <= 1) is excluded for every design built so far, at every budget, by factors of 2.5-100 in F.

### A2. The case against

1. **No theorem.** Every number above is a floor of a closure *we* designed in two days, or a curve from one integration scheme (thresholds, then walks). The exponent 0.7 rests on three points. A smarter integral of the same closure (exact 3-D piecewise-linear pieces per class, shared boundaries, smooth probabilistic verdicts) could have a steeper curve; nobody has measured the *information ceiling* of n weight-chosen evaluations (B1 does).
2. **The floors keep falling with exact dimensions** (.51 -> .39 -> .24) and T298's best closure has a cross-model sd of only ~.12 pp; its +.21 pp mean is systematic, and H-E10 (60/60 positive) says the sign is universal. A deductive argument for that sign and rough size is not absurd.
3. **The estimators rank the models** (corr .96) and their residual sd is 1.0-1.7 pp, so 89% of the variance is already captured by a 4k-26k FPE deductive computation. The gap to strict is a factor 1.2-4 in RMS, not 100.
4. **ARC's practice is lenient.** Their own M_4,3 estimators lost to sampling 2x in RMSE and were called "roughly match". By that standard the challenge is nearly met: T300 (F 5.97, caveats) and T302 (F 20.6) bracket the lenient line, and T304 targets F ~ 8 deductively.
5. **Random-weight successes** (Winer; Christiano's conjecture) show the architecture is not the obstacle; the obstacle is a representation faithful to a scalar-innovation mixture. That is a research problem, not an impossibility.

### A3. What would settle it

No experiment settles "no cheap deductive estimator of any kind" (CAPP-type hardness is out of reach). What settles the *practical* question is the **information-ceiling test** (B1): if even an unrestricted fitted function of everything 1-2 evaluations per class can reveal cannot reach .85 pp on held-out retrains, no deductive function of it can, and the cheap strict regime closes for all evaluation-based designs. If it can, the task becomes deriving that function. For the lenient question: the sealed HP2 score of a deductive, cancellation-free estimator (T302/T304 lineage).

## B. Five new approaches

**B1. Information-ceiling test (ICT).** *Computation (diagnostic, inductive by construction).* For each DEV2 model and each of the 720 classes, record everything the existing T300/T302 anchors already compute: the R0 verdict, margin, tangent slope along g23, Jacobian norm w.r.t. the lows, Newton threshold, gate pattern. Build <= 10 universal summaries (sum of threshold masses, sums of Phi(-m/s) at 3 fixed scales, counts of classes with margin < c, the rank_exact and probe-v1 outputs). Fit a <= 5-parameter linear predictor of accuracy on 40 DEV2 models; test on the other 40 and on the 5 LB3 seeds. This also answers the brief's (i): the two opposite-sign estimators are two of the features. Arithmetic says (i) alone cannot pass: with sds 1.06 and 1.73 pp and the bias-cancelling weight .70 (fitted, so not allowed anyway), the combined sd is sqrt(.82 + .77 rho) pp; only rho <= -.8 would reach .43 pp. *Cost.* ~2 CPU-h; features exist. *Decisive test.* Held-out RMS. *Go:* <= .6 pp with <= 5 parameters: the information is in 1-2 evaluations per class, fund the deductive derivation of those parameters. *Stop:* > 1.0 pp: close cheap strict for evaluation-based designs (scoped) and stop funding one-point-per-class ideas.

**B2. Prefix-tree and homogeneity sharing (brief ii).** *Computation.* For a point closure the inputs of all 720 classes at one skeleton value are sequences over {mu, v1, v2, v3} with each v used once; the distinct prefixes number sum_t sum_k C(3,k) t!/(t-k)! = 3,145 step-evaluations against 7,200 for 720 separate passes: 2.3x on anchors and tangents alike (2.5x for top-4). Along a walk, the state before the varying position is unchanged: ~1.4x. All-low prefixes are a 1-D family by homogeneity (h_t(mu 1) = |mu| h_t(-1)), free. *Cost.* Implementation only; it moves T304's target F ~ 8 to ~4-6 and T302's 26k to ~11-19k. *Test.* Instrumented FLOPs before/after. *Stop:* < 1.8x on anchor designs. It cannot reach strict (needs 20x), and T305 is already mapping position-sharing; this is the exact-sharing complement.

**B3. Future-aware mixture propagation (brief iii: ARC's methods, done faithfully).** *Why E35 failed.* The innovation per step is one scalar, so step-1 units are perfectly correlated and off-diagonal cumulants are O(1), not O(n^-1/2); the Hermite diagram truncation has no small parameter at width 16, and the argmax readout is not a 1-D threshold. *What a faithful port needs.* (a) A scalar-innovation-exact ReLU step: given the state law as a Gaussian mixture, x_t enters through <= 16 breakpoints, so each component splits into <= 17 truncated pieces with closed-form moments, no Hermite truncation. (b) A projection chosen by ARC's sketching theorem: merge components only along directions the *future* readout does not read, i.e. keep separate those whose pre-images of the rival margins (backward adjoint of W_o through the likely late gates, as the observer computes) differ in sign. (c) A fixed component budget B. *Cost.* ~B x 17 x 16^2 x 10 FLOPs; B = 500 is 3.7k FPE. *Decisive cheapest test.* With *sampled* component laws at step 8 (diagnostic), how many importance-merged components per class reproduce the per-unit P(correct) within 10%? T295 says 698-3,518 branches per blind unit over the last 2-3 steps. *Stop:* > 50 per class (then B >= 36k, cost >= 1e5 FPE, and the readout mixture problem of T294/T295 returns). Expected to stop; included because it is the one way ARC's own toolkit could be made honest here, and the test is cheap.

**B4. Stationarity identities as the only legitimate "advice from the family" (brief iv).** A fitted offset or a regression on weight statistics is "observing correlations empirically" (Hilton, 2026-02-04) and ARC would reject it; also moot, since even an oracle offset leaves F 14-40. What is *not* sampled: every retrain is a near-stationary point of the same expected loss, so 432 integral identities hold for free, e.g. E[(softmax(L) - onehot(y)) h^T] ~ 0 and, contracting with W_o, E[<p, L>] = E[L_y], a calibration identity coupling the error set to a smooth functional. *Computation.* None per model beyond what the estimator already computes; the question is whether any constrained functional pins an accuracy-adjacent quantity deductively. *Cheapest test.* On DEV2 (diagnostic sampling) measure the readout-gradient residual relative to its sampling floor (how stationary are fused/torch retrains under AdamW?) and corr(E[<p,L>] - E[L_y], accuracy). *Stop:* residual > 10% of a single-batch gradient, or corr < .5. Odds ~5%; it is a first-principles lever nobody has looked at, and the training procedure is part of what ARC hands over with the retrain protocol. Separate note for T203: the *noise* protocol is gameable by a Taylor expansion around the clean model with a sampled gradient amortised over copies, so any claimed noise-protocol solution should be checked for that; the retrain protocol is the honest one.

**B5. Exact marginalisation of the last input inside any closure (brief v: change what is integrated).** For every skeleton point where x9 is a low, replace its pinned value by its exact conditional law N(0,1)|<v_k pushed through the last layer only: the verdict as a function of x9 is piecewise constant with <= 16 gate breakpoints plus argmax changes, so P(correct | prefix) is an exact 1-D integral at ~0.1 FPE. The closure's output becomes a probability in one dimension instead of a 0/1 verdict, which also smooths the walk integrand. T298's variant (e) (true x9 kept) cut R0's floor 13%; this is its deductive form at near-zero cost. *Test.* Oracle floor of "top-k + x9 exact law" vs the same closure on 10 DEV2 models (10 CPU-min). *Stop:* < 10% floor reduction. A lever for T304, not a strict route. On the brief's wider (v): the only mechanism with an exact mass *and* a deductively boundable rate is the blind region (11/1024 x [0,1], midpoint error <= .54 pp, measured .27); the soft-accuracy decomposition (indicator = softmax + boundary residual) does not help because the residual's expansion coefficients are the margin density at zero, the very thing we cannot compute.

## C. Our honest claim today

On 80 open retrains of the zoo configuration, every deductive estimator we built ranks the models (corr .96 with the truth) but misses the strict own-cost bar by factors of 5-100 in F, and the misses are floors, not budgets: frozen-gate classes leave >= .9 pp bias at any cost (verified), Gaussian laws leave -11 pp at class level, linear relaxations decide nothing at T=10 (verified), exact enumeration needs >= 1.35M and probably 1e10 regions, and point closures of the low inputs have floors of .24-.51 pp whose deterministic integrals cost 20-200k FPE. One cheap design (R0 with a one-anchor Newton threshold, 1.75k FPE) reaches the lenient bar on DEV2 (F 5.97), but one step is inductive and its accuracy is partly cancellation; the deductive, cancellation-free version is at F 20.6, and a top-4 version aiming at F ~ 8 is in progress. If asked by ARC: we have no deductive estimator that matches sampling; we have one that comes within 2-6x of it in MSE on retrains at ~2-26k FPE, with every step labelled, and we have measured why the gap exists. We believe the strict bar at cheap budgets is unreachable for every design family tried, and we have no proof.

## D. Odds and the single next experiment

Subjective. Strict cheap (F <= 1 at 1-4k FPE) on sealed HP2 within 3 months: **5%** (was 15%; T292-T302 all landed where the pessimistic reading predicted). Deductive, cancellation-free lenient pass on sealed HP2 within 3 months: **50%**. Strict at any cost via the exact route: **10%**, decided by T270's g_1e-7. Anyone, strict, within a year: **10%**. ARC's reported solution meets strict on retrains: **15%**; meets their own "roughly match": **60%**.

**Next experiment: B1, the information-ceiling test**, pre-registered. Features: the per-class quantities already produced by the T300/T302 anchors on 80 DEV2 models (R0 verdict, margin, g23 slope, low-Jacobian norm, Newton threshold) plus rank_exact and probe-v1 outputs; <= 10 fixed summaries; <= 5 fitted parameters; fit on DEV2 models 0-39, test on 40-79 and the 5 LB3 seeds. **Go line:** held-out RMS <= .60 pp. Then the cheap strict regime is information-feasible and the work is to derive those parameters from the weights (B4's identities are one place to look). **Stop line:** held-out RMS > 1.0 pp. Then no deductive function of 1-2 evaluations per class can pass strict; freeze the best deductive lenient estimator (T304 lineage, with B2 and B5), send it to T281 for HP2, and report the result to the owner and ARC as the project's claim. Cost ~2 CPU-h; it could fail, and either outcome changes what we do next.
