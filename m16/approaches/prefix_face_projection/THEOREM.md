# Unbounded projection and sufficient invariance

For positive target u, positive homogeneity of the bias-free recurrent ReLU network allows dividing every input/state by u and setting the target to1. A negative prefix produces state h1=(s,0), where s=Wi0*x0/u>0 because Wi0<0. Its rank relation to the target is fixed. The remaining rank domain consists of the two unbounded sides x<=1<=y and y<=1<=x. Closed sides are conservative overapproximations; strict-boundary Gaussian probabilities vanish.

Take every original moving final-gate/readout boundary from the T147 extended gate words. For each occurrence and rank side include the T159 independently checked closed crossing constraints and s>=0. The two nonzero-input-normal coordinates permit equality elimination with no inequality sign reversal. The remaining rows have form a*s+b*t+c>=0. Rows with b=0 remain; every pair b>0,B<0 generates (a/b−A/B)*s+(c/b−C/B)>=0. These are positive linear combinations of original rows. Conversely, these pairwise inequalities imply every t lower bound is below every upper bound. If only one side exists, a finite t can be chosen freely beyond it. Thus Fourier–Motzkin gives the exact closed projection, including unbounded intervals and points.

Union all occurrence/side projections. On any connected open component of its complement in s>0, no relevant moving correctness boundary can be crossed for any fixed (x,y) in the rank domain. The inherited continuity/tie argument therefore makes correctness pointwise constant in s except input hyperplanes of Gaussian measure zero. A moving row with zero input normal remains unknown, blocking an invariance conclusion. A zero state-derivative row is independent of s and does not constitute a moving boundary. No local gate-word exclusions or input cutoff are used.

Complement intervals are sufficient no-boundary regions, not maximal actual invariance regions: some included final-gate faces may never change correctness, and separate boundary contributions may cancel after integration. Covering all s would therefore be inconclusive, not a proof that the pair value varies everywhere. All interval lists describe OPEN complement components; listed endpoints are excluded. They may include finite isolated projection points that split two otherwise open intervals.

If an interval survives, it can in principle remove prefix dependence there for each fixed positive u. The Gaussian prefix probability would then be integrated over its weight-derived ratio interval, while the constant pair value still depends on u. Neither that constant nor the resulting Gaussian contribution is evaluated in this task. The negative-target sector, other prefix/rank sectors, and global accuracy remain separate.
