# Conditional active directions and pair-interaction ceiling

## Question

Can the low-input dependence of a tie boundary be reduced to two or three linear
coordinates, or to pair interactions, without erasing rare wide error bands?

## Method

The proposed calculation conditions on the top-three positions and values, leaving
seven independent upper-truncated Gaussian inputs. Exact backpropagation gives the
tie-margin gradient from the weights. Its second-moment eigenvectors identify
candidate active directions. A rank-two reduction would permit adaptive exact
two-dimensional gate-cell integration instead of enumerating ten-dimensional cones.

Two alternative explanations are specified **before experiments**:

1. A dominant average gradient could hide substantial gate-dependent fluctuations.
   Compare uncentered and centered gradient spectra, then test held-out polynomial
   predictions using the first two/three directions.
2. An apparently additive law could fail only through low-order interactions, or
   through broad-band topology that no signed-offset approximation captures.
   Fit both additive and pairwise Legendre expansions to diagnostic signed offsets;
   evaluate their absolute-offset integrals and compare with exact full band mass
   on independent inputs. This is an oracle ceiling test, not an estimator.

All diagnostic inputs are random and explicitly excluded from a deductive claim.
Only DEV weight copies generated by the exact `leaderboard.models(dev=True)` noise
rule are evaluated; cached original weights are only the base of those copies.
No test-model accuracy or leaderboard truth is consulted.

## Status

Done, negative as a competitive estimator. The exact multiclass-envelope diagnostic
identifies a useful obstruction. No estimator or solution claimed.

`results.json` contains six rank-2/3 position/value configurations, with 8192
training and 8192 held-out lower-input vectors per configuration, evaluated on
`dev_s0.0025_0` and `dev_s0.02_0`. The positions/top values are fixed across the two
models. These are **conditional chamber diagnostics, not whole-model accuracy
estimates**. Neither six-chamber average below uses global chamber probability
weights. Random inputs and regressions serve diagnosis only.

The results separate three obstacles:

1. **No two/three-dimensional exact reduction.** On the mild DEV copy, the first
   three gradient eigenvectors carry 64.4–97.2% of gradient energy; a cubic in those
   coordinates explains only 3.9–55.9% of held-out tie-margin variance. Gate-dependent
   gradient directions cannot be discarded without a substantial residual. This
   does not rule out a nonlinear coordinate system or a nonsmooth active-space model.
2. **Pairwise offset fits retain bias.** An additive Legendre oracle uses 36
   coefficients; adding all pair interactions through degree three uses 225.
   Relative bias in the sum of held-out absolute-offset masses is −4.88%/−4.02%
   (additive/pair) on the mild copy and −6.48%/−4.01% on the harsh copy. Increasing
   the earlier exploratory training size from 2048 to 8192 did not remove the
   pairwise bias. This is a finite-degree fit result, not an impossibility theorem
   for arbitrary pair functions.
3. **A single tied-pair margin does not give total error.** The exact nearest
   pair-crossing mass divided by full error-band mass is 92.84% mild and 29.09%
   harsh in these six-chamber sums. A diagnostic readout ablation replaces all
   other logits by the midpoint of the tied pair's logits. Its exact error mass
   agrees almost perfectly with the nearest crossing on the mild copy. In the
   harsh copy, disconnected pair intervals account for only 0.30% of full error
   mass, while adding the other logits accounts for 70.60%. This is a comparison
   of analytic event sets after a model modification, not a causal measurement
   of naturally winning classes.

`envelope.py` resolves that last distinction by solving the winning interval for
**every original affine logit in every gate cell**, then attributing its exact
Gaussian mass to the input rank of the winner. No model modification is used.
For each affine logit k, solve all inequalities
`L_k(t)-L_j(t) >= 0` (with first-index argmax tie-breaking), intersect with the gate
cell, split at the input tie, and integrate the resulting interval analytically.
The input ordering is fixed on each side of the rank-2/3 tie.

| DEV copy | Max wins | Rank 3 wins | Rank >=4 wins | Linear-at-tie / exact mass |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| sigma .0025 | 3.54% | 88.53% | 7.93% | 1.0173 |
| sigma .02 | 41.49% | 10.33% | 48.18% | 0.9142 |

These are fractions of error mass in the six conditional chambers, using 2048
fresh lower-input vectors each. In the mild copy, rank 4 alone contributes 6.56%.
One mild chamber has 27.12% of its error from ranks >=4 and five competitor ranks
each contribute at least 1% of its error. Three harsh chambers have 8–9 meaningful
competitor ranks. A missing-max correction is therefore insufficient in this probe.

The cheap comparison freezes the gates at the tie, extends **all** logits linearly,
and solves the full multiclass upper envelope on the entire segment. Its per-chamber
mass ratios are 0.871–1.326 (mild) and 0.868–1.099 (harsh), despite the fairly close
aggregate mild ratio. That apparent aggregate success can be cancellation and
cannot be extrapolated to accuracy. Cheap tests of the two alternatives are now
available: rank decomposition tests missing-max-only explanations, while exact
versus frozen envelopes isolate gate movement from omitted competitor logits.

**The promising six-chamber aggregate does not survive the complete face law.**
Before expanding the test we checked prior implementations: E15's
`tube.F_faces(K=1, extrap=True)` already intersects all logit inequalities on the
finite wedge. Thus this frozen-envelope formula is the same approximation family,
not a new idea. E15 uses radial integration and a separate blind region; here we
use Gaussian line integration and complete signed rank-2/3 and rank-1/2 faces.
Neither change fixes frozen gates. E24's linear band-edge response across low
inputs is a different extrapolation.

`full_face.py` applies the frozen formula unchanged to predeclared fixed Sobol
rules (seeds 741033/741034), paired with exact walks at the same nodes. Both face
families are weighted by their exact model-free co-area constants. This is an
independent **fixed-node quadrature diagnostic**, not a candidate mechanistic
estimator. The paired exact-node value has finite quadrature error and is not
called ground truth.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| sigma .0025 | 1024 | 5.9817% | 15.8106% | −9.8289 pp |
| sigma .0025 | 4096 | 5.9939% | 15.7266% | −9.7326 pp |
| sigma .02 | 1024 | 38.0376% | 41.0021% | −2.9645 pp |

This independently reproduces the broad failure of the E15 one-cell extrapolation.
The favorable six-chamber aggregate was unrepresentative. It does not justify
further development of the same frozen formula.

**Direct pairwise full-band integration also fails.** After the first oracle probe,
we tested an anchored decomposition of the *full* band mass F, avoiding a single
offset entirely. If seven low-input CDF coordinates are anchored at 1/2, its
second-order integral is

`sum_{i<j} E[F(U_i,U_j,anchor)] - 5 sum_i E[F(U_i,anchor)] + 15 F(anchor)`.

This follows exactly from truncating the anchored ANOVA decomposition after pairs;
no fitted constants are used. The one-/two-dimensional integrals use Gauss–Legendre
nodes. Refining 5→9→17→33 nodes does not repair omitted interactions: mild case 2
converges near 0.00103 versus true 0.01942 (−94.7%); case 4 gives 0.02670 versus
0.01920 (+39.1%). The 9-node case-2 approximation is even negative. See
`refinement.json`. More accurate pair integrals cannot by themselves repair this
anchored truncation. The hierarchy need not preserve nonnegativity at finite order.

**Checks.** The exact Jacobian agrees with centered finite differences to 8.42e−8.
Two exact full-band integrals agree with independent 65,536-bin line grids to
8.73e−7 and 9.11e−6. The multiclass-envelope decomposition agrees with a separately
implemented exact segment walker to below 3e−13 and its total rank mass equals the
complete line-window measure to below 1e−6 on every diagnostic line.

## Compute accounting

All model calls and fitted linear algebra in this probe are diagnostic overhead.
Historical segment-walker FPE counts are not reused: the old `tube.walk_side`
quantity named `cells` actually contains discounted hypothetical incremental costs.
Instead the final probe instruments actual JVP input counts and uses the new
walker's true cell counts. No hypothetical incremental saving is claimed.

The final main probe used 196,608 full Jacobian inputs (conservative recurrence
allowance 2,162,688 FPE), 1,533,777 exact full-mass cells and 517,558 JVP inputs
(4,102,670 full recurrence FPE). CPU time inside the script: 9.23 seconds. The
additional exact rank-envelope diagnostic used 356,454 cells/JVP inputs, 712,908
recurrence FPE and 2.28 CPU seconds. Validation used 131,072 grid forwards and
80 finite-difference forwards. Pair-integral refinement used 529,010 cells
(1,058,020 recurrence FPE), 2.06 CPU seconds. The complete-face replication used
297,418 recurrence FPE and 0.80 CPU seconds. Prefixes at 1024 reuse the 4096-node
calculation; they are not additional model work. Detailed counts are in JSON files.
These FPE figures are **not complete leaderboard costs**: polynomial fitting,
10x10 envelope comparisons, weight scans and transformations are timed but not
assigned portable FLOP counts because no estimator is proposed. Full CPU time
also includes model-free Legendre features and truncated-normal transforms.
Exploratory 2048-sample, 8192-sample and accounting-corrected runs preceded the
final main run; total work remained well below the authorized 300 CPU seconds,
single-threaded. Imports/startup are excluded from in-script CPU measurements.

## Claims

- [measured] Two/three gradient directions and the tested cubic approximation do
  not reconstruct the mild DEV tie margins accurately in these chambers.
- [measured] The tested pairwise offset expansion retains roughly 4% relative
  mass bias in both six-chamber sums.
- [measured] Both max and rank>=4 competitors contribute error beyond the tied
  pair; their balance varies strongly by chamber and noise level.
- [measured] The pairwise anchored full-band hierarchy can be severely biased even
  after one-/two-dimensional integration is refined.
- [inferred] A useful next reduction must preserve a multiclass boundary envelope,
  rather than only improve the distribution of one pair's crossing.
- [speculative] A model-derived compact upper-envelope representation might be
  easier than representing every gate, but this experiment has not constructed it.

## Alternatives not ruled out

Finite polynomial degree can miss a low-dimensional nonsmooth function. Signed
offsets can miss disconnected errors. A small set of chambers cannot establish
whole-model accuracy. Oracle learning success would not supply a deductive way to
compute expansion coefficients.

Only two noisy DEV copies were used, not independently trained seeds. Exact
gradient second moments are Monte Carlo diagnostics. Sparse nonlinear active
coordinates, alternate anchors, third/higher-order interactions, direct learned
envelopes, and exact multiclass polyhedral compression were not ruled out. The
next task worth registering is **DEV multiclass-envelope compression**: preserve
the relevant competitor logits and gate-dependent changes; success requires
independent chamber-level validation and an honest model-dependent cost ledger.
Any such task must change how gate movement is represented: freezing the envelope
at the tie has now failed on the complete DEV face law as well as in E15.

## Failure modes

High conditional gradient rank prevents a cheap two-dimensional exact reduction.
Moment/absolute-value errors need not predict accuracy errors. Polynomial fits can
overfit or have tail bias. This study cannot certify a global residual bound.
