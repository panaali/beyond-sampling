# T421: scale study, lead (grid of 2nd-largest RNNs beyond (16,10))

↑ [Approaches](../README.md) · [TASKS](../../../TASKS.md) · [INDEX](../../../INDEX.md) · owner request: [OWNER_QA 6.7](../../../OWNER_QA.md#q6-7)

**Pre-registration: committed before any grid training starts (2026-10-19).** Sections marked *(filled later)* are
written after the runs; nothing above them changes after training starts except by a dated, flagged amendment.

## Question

Do our positive results on M_16,10 (H-E16 walk quadrature, H-R1 rare-event estimators and the graded rule, H-R2 / H-R3
locate and repair, certified-pruning survivor growth, the T401 safe-use rating) keep holding as the network grows in
width H, in sequence length T, or both? This folder produces the model grid and its registry; the per-method tests are
separate sessions (spawned by the DRI), each with its own pre-registered "holds" criterion.

## Method (pre-registered)

**Grid.** H in {16, 24, 32, 48, 64} x T in {10, 15, 20}; 15 points. Same task as M_16,10: x ~ N(0, I_T), label = position
of the 2nd-largest input (`alg_zoo.tasks`, `x.argsort(-1)[..., -2]`), T-way output. Architecture alg_zoo `DistRNN`
(bias-free ReLU RNN, h_0 = 0, h_t = relu(W_ih x_t + W_hh h_{t-1}), logits = W_out h_T).

**Recipe and schedule.** `alg_zoo.training.train("2nd_argmax", hidden_size=H, seq_len=T, seed=seed, n_val=2**20)`, i.e.
the zoo's own training code with every argument at its zoo default (AdamW, weight decay 1e-3, lr 0.04/sqrt(H), 2^14
warm-up steps, exponential decay to 0.01x by mid-run, batch 1024, n_train = 2^30 fresh samples, torch.manual_seed(seed)),
the "30s" setting of T86 ([ladder_training/train.py](https://github.com/panaali/clean-neurons/blob/claude/t86-ladder-training/m16/approaches/ladder_training/train.py),
which is the same call). n_val only sets the size of the post-training validation pass and never touches the weights.
CPU, one thread per run (benchmarked: a second thread does not help at these sizes). alg_zoo installed editable from a
clone of github.com/alignment-research-center/alg-zoo (AGENTS.md Environment). Script: [train.py](train.py).

**Seeds and streams.** Primary panel per point: **cpu seeds 0-5** (6 models), all trained as above on CPU. ARC's zoo was
trained on GPU, so zoo seed s and our cpu seed s are different random draws of the same recipe; the zoo's published
models exist at two grid points, (16,10) and (32,10), seeds 0-4, and are registered as a **supplementary "zoo" panel**
(downloaded from storage.googleapis.com/arc-ml-public/alg/zoo; a public read, not contact). Note: the zoo publishes the
best of 5 seeds, and the project's M_16,10 is ARC's separate blog example (accuracy .953), not a zoo seed, so it is a
favourable draw: the zoo's own (16,10) seeds reach .880-.948 and T392's CPU seed 0 .884.

**Reuse of T392.** T392's k = 1 models (branch claude/t392-chunked-rnn, chunked_rnn/models/k1h{16,24,32}_s*.json) are
reused as cpu seeds (16,10) s0, (24,10) s0-2, (32,10) s0-2. Check that they match the recipe exactly: T392's
re-implementation and `alg_zoo.training.train` on CPU, both run for 2^18 samples at H = 24, seed 3, give bit-identical
weights (max |dW| = 0; same module construction order, same `th.randn(batch, T)` data stream, same schedule). Script:
[import_existing.py](import_existing.py).

**Accuracy.** Plain MC on 1e7 N(0,1) inputs, float64 numpy forward, inputs common to all models of the same T
(`np.random.default_rng([421, T])`, drawn in sequential chunks), so models at one point are paired; s.e. <= 1e-4. Script:
[evaluate.py](evaluate.py).

**Point rule (drop / keep), fixed now.**
- A point is **kept** if >= 3 of its 6 cpu seeds reach accuracy >= .90 (so a panel of >= 3 good models exists).
  The anchor (16,10) is kept regardless.
- **Screen** (to save compute on hopeless points): seeds 2-5 of a point are not trained if cpu seeds 0 and 1 are both done
  and both below .80 (status "screened"). Implemented in [screen.py](screen.py), checked before each job.
- A point with all 6 seeds done and < 3 at >= .90 is **dropped**. Dropped and screened points are reported in the
  registry with all their models and weights; they are not hidden, only flagged.
- Per-method sessions score the primary panel (all 6 cpu seeds of a kept point; models below .90 included and
  flagged) and may add a secondary score on the >= .90 seeds only; the zoo panel is a supplementary check where it exists.

**Registry format.** [registry.json](registry.json) (`models`: tag, H, T, seed, stream, acc, se, pth, npz,
sha256_npz_weights, source; `points`: status and accuracy summary) and a readable [registry.md](registry.md), both
generated by [build_registry.py](build_registry.py) from the per-model records results/models/<tag>.json (which also hold
wall time, torch version and the 2^20 validation accuracy). Weights: `weights/m_<H>_<T>_<stream><seed>.pth` (alg_zoo
DistRNN state_dict, `alg_zoo.loading.load_dist_rnn` loads it) and `weights/m_<H>_<T>_<stream><seed>.npz` (Wi (H,),
Wh (H,H), Wo (T,H) float64, the T86 ladder_training format). Loader: [models.py](models.py)
(`load(H, T, seed, stream="cpu") -> Wi, Wh, Wo`, `load_torch`, `panel(H, T)`).

**Execution.** 83 new runs (90 minus the 7 T392 reuses), est. ~246 core-hours (benchmark: 1.0 h for (16,10) up to
6.2 h for (64,20), single thread). Queues: [queues/](queues/) q00 (lead container: (32,10) s3-5 and (16,15) s0-5, the
first new points) and q01-q12 (12 helper child sessions, own containers), longest-processing-time balanced, ~10-11 h
wall. Each container runs its queue two jobs at a time ([run_queue.sh](run_queue.sh), [job.sh](job.sh); <= 2 heavy
processes per container, each under `ulimit -v 2500000`), and every finished model is committed and pushed on its own.
GPU (Colab, cap 15 units) only if CPU turns out to be the bottleneck.

**Prediction [speculative], fixed now.** All T = 10 points with H >= 24 are kept (T392: H24/H32 seeds .97-.99); accuracy
falls with T at fixed H; (16,15) and (16,20) are at risk of drop/screen; at H >= 32 every T is kept.

## Status

**Done (2026-10-19, ~15:00-23:00 UTC).** 85 cpu models exist (78 trained here, 7 reused from T392): 90 grid slots minus 5 seeds
skipped by the pre-registered screen ((16,15) s3-5, (16,20) s4-5); 13 of 15 points kept, 2 screened. Deviations from the pre-registration, none of which changes a weight or an accuracy:
- **Evaluation chunking.** evaluate.py was changed from 1e6-row to 250k-row chunks after H = 64 runs hit MemoryError under
  `ulimit -v 2500000`. The input stream is identical (sequential `standard_normal` draws; checked with `np.array_equal`).
- **Save-step failures, recovered from checkpoints.** 24 runs trained to the end but failed at the save step (first job of
  each helper container: `weights/` did not exist yet; H = 64: the MemoryError above). [recover.py](recover.py) re-saves
  them from the run's final alg_zoo checkpoint and final train-log line; it reproduces an ordinary record exactly
  (m_32_10_cpu3: same accuracy and weight hash). Their records say "re-saved by recover.py". Two helpers relaunched
  queues into the same run dirs before the fix; alg_zoo's logs are append-only, so the finished runs' final lines survived.
  m_64_15_cpu2/3 were retrained from scratch (CPU training is deterministic, so this is the same model).
- **Screen timing.** (16,20) cpu2 and cpu3 were trained before cpu1's record existed (cpu1 had failed at the save step), so
  the screen could not fire for them; they are kept in the registry as extra models of a screened point.
- **Load balancing.** The lead container took (16,10) s2-3 from helper q06's tail ([queues/q00b.txt](queues/q00b.txt)).

- Accuracy rises with H and falls with T. The error rate 1 - acc spans ~30x across the kept grid: ~.09 at (16,10) and

## Claims

- [measured] The 85-model cpu grid and the 10 zoo models are registered with weights in both formats and 1e7-MC
  accuracies; 13 points meet the pre-registered keep rule, (16,15) and (16,20) are screened (seeds 0-1 both < .80).
- [measured] T392's k = 1 models are bit-identical to `alg_zoo.training.train` on CPU (2^18-sample check, max |dW| = 0),
  and our accuracies for them match T392's to 1e-4 (independent evaluation code and inputs).
- [measured] CPU training is reproducible across containers: (16,10) cpu2 and cpu3 were trained twice, in the lead
  container and in helper q06's, with identical weights and accuracies (only wall time differed).
- [measured] At T = 10, typical H = 16 retrains reach .82-.95 (median .906 over our 6, .910 over the zoo's 5);
  M_16,10's .953 is above all 11.
- [inferred] For per-method sessions: the grid's error rates are 3-30x lower than M_16,10's except at (24,15), (24,20)
  and (32,20), so methods whose cost scales with 1/error (rare events) or whose bars are relative (F) face very
  different regimes across points; compare at matched error rate as well as matched (H, T).
- [inferred] The training recipe, not capacity alone, limits small-H long-T points: (16,15) and (16,20) stall at
  .63-.73 and ~.46 with all seeds, a plateau, not seed noise.

## Alternatives not ruled out

- The keep/screen outcomes are for the zoo schedule (2^30 samples, lr 0.04/sqrt(H)) only. (16,15), (16,20) and the
  weak seeds at (24,20) might reach .9 with longer training or another lr; not tried (out of scope: the study fixes the
  zoo recipe).
- CPU vs GPU stream: our cpu seeds and ARC's GPU zoo seeds are different draws of the same recipe; at (16,10) and (32,10)
  the two panels overlap, but a systematic CPU/GPU difference at other points is not excluded (only two zoo points exist).
- Six seeds per point give a coarse view of the seed distribution; the zoo's best-of-5 selection means ARC's published
  models (and M_16,10) sit in the upper tail, so a method that "holds" on our panels may still behave differently on
  the best-of-5 model, and vice versa.
- The recovered models' wall times come from the train logs; their weights come from the same final checkpoint file
  train.py would have used (no evidence of any difference; one direct check).

## Failure modes

- Save-step fragility: the job script assumed `weights/` existed and that evaluation fits in 2.5 GB at H = 64; both
  failed and are now handled (folders created, 250k-row chunks, automatic recovery from the checkpoint).
- Queue relaunches: launching a second runner into the same run dirs restarts finished jobs; launch.sh now refuses while
  a runner is alive.
- The screen relies on git being current in each container; a lost or late record lets screened seeds run anyway
  (harmless, extra models).
