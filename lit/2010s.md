# Literature, 2010–2019: methods for estimating the accuracy of a ReLU RNN mechanistically

**Setup.** Accuracy = Gaussian measure of a union of polyhedral cones in R^10 (the network is piecewise linear and positively homogeneous). Errors sit in thin bands near ties among the top order statistics, plus a region where at most one input is positive.
Gap codes: (1) Gaussian measure of polyhedra, (2) region enumeration and verification, (3) rare events, (4) QMC, control variates and MLMC for discontinuous integrands, (5) moment propagation, (6) order statistics.

Items are ranked by how promising they are.

---

## 1. Botev (2017): minimax exponential tilting for truncated multivariate normals
- **Citation:** Z. I. Botev, "The normal law under linear restrictions: simulation and estimation via minimax tilting," *JRSS-B* 79(1):125–148, 2017. arXiv:1603.04166. Code: R `TruncatedNormal`, MATLAB `mvNcdf`/`mvrandn`, Python ports (e.g. `truncnorm`/`minimax-tilting` repos).
- **Method:** Estimates P(l ≤ AX ≤ u) for X ~ N(0, Σ) with an importance sampler built on Genz's sequential-conditioning (separation-of-variables) proposal. The proposal is exponentially tilted, and the tilt comes from a convex minimax problem. The estimator has vanishing relative error in the rare-event limit and also produces a certified upper bound. In d ≈ 10 a few thousand samples typically give relative error below 1e-3.
- **Gaps:** (1), and (3) through its rare-event efficiency.
- **Application here:** For each activation region R (a cone {G_R x ≥ 0} given by the gate signs over all 10 steps), the correct-output subset is a further cone {G_R x ≥ 0, (W_o^{(c)} − W_o^{(j)}) M_R x ≥ 0 for all j}, where c = the 2nd-largest index, also linear given the rank order. Each piece is a polyhedral cone with roughly 10–60 constraints. Botev's method works with A of more rows than columns once you reduce to a minimal H-representation. Run it on the error cones only: their total mass is about 4.7%, they are thin, and they are exactly where crude MC wastes samples. Then accuracy = 1 − Σ error-cone masses.

## 2. Preintegration / conditional MC along one direction + randomized QMC (Griewank, Kuo, Leövey & Sloan 2018; Dick, Kuo & Sloan 2013)
- **Citation:** A. Griewank, F. Y. Kuo, H. Leövey, I. H. Sloan, "High dimensional integration of kinks and jumps — smoothing by preintegration," *J. Comput. Appl. Math.* 344:259–274, 2018, arXiv:1712.00920. Also Griebel, Kuo & Sloan, "The smoothing effect of integration in R^d and the ANOVA decomposition," *Math. Comp.* 2013. Survey: J. Dick, F. Y. Kuo, I. H. Sloan, "High-dimensional integration: the quasi-Monte Carlo way," *Acta Numerica* 22:133–288, 2013.
- **Method:** Integrate one carefully chosen variable out exactly so the jump becomes a smooth function of the remaining d−1 variables. Then apply (randomized) QMC, which can approach O(N^{-1}) error instead of O(N^{-1/2}). Smoothness needs monotonicity in the chosen variable. Gilbert, Kuo & Sloan (2021, arXiv:2112.11621) show that without it you get square-root singularities. These are still integrable and much better than jumps.
- **Gaps:** (4), (6).
- **Application here:** Take a ray or line x = y + t·u with y ~ N(0, I) restricted to u^⊥, where u is e.g. a random unit vector or the (1,…,1)/√10 direction. Along the line the network is exactly piecewise linear in t. Breakpoints come from tracking gate flips, which is about 160 pre-activations each with linear dependence on t inside a region, so they can be walked exactly. The correct-set is then a finite union of intervals in t, and its N(0,1) mass is an exact sum of Φ differences. This conditional estimator is Rao–Blackwellized, so its variance is never larger than crude MC and is usually much smaller, because the "thin band" errors become smooth contributions. Feed the outer 9-dim y from scrambled Sobol' points (Owen scrambling) for further gains. This is the cheapest high-payoff idea.
  - Related: **directional sampling**. Positive homogeneity makes radial integration trivial, so integrate over 2D great circles instead: x = r(cos θ a + sin θ b). This is an exact arc-length computation per random 2-plane, the "two-dimensional preintegration" variant. Arcs of the correct set come from angles where linear forms vanish, and the angular measure is uniform for the 2D Gaussian.

## 3. Exact region enumeration with MILP (Serra, Tjandraatmadja & Ramalingam 2018) + polyhedral-complex traversal (Jordan, Lewis & Dimakis 2019)
- **Citation:** T. Serra, C. Tjandraatmadja, S. Ramalingam, "Bounding and Counting Linear Regions of Deep Neural Networks," *ICML 2018*, arXiv:1711.02114. M. Jordan, J. Lewis, A. G. Dimakis, "Provable Certificates for Adversarial Examples: Fitting a Ball in the Union of Polytopes" (GeoCert), *NeurIPS 2019*, arXiv:1903.08778, code github.com/revbucket/geometric-certificates.
- **Method:** Serra et al. encode a ReLU net as a MILP and count or enumerate feasible activation patterns (one-tree / solution-pool enumeration). GeoCert shows that the linear regions form a polyhedral complex. It traverses neighboring regions across shared facets, solving one LP per facet and checking which ReLU constraints are tight.
- **Gaps:** (2), and (1) when combined with item 1.
- **Application here:** Unrolled, the RNN is a 10-layer, 16-wide ReLU net on R^10, about 160 neurons. Hanin & Rolnick (item 9) suggest the reachable patterns number far fewer than the worst case. Restrict to the unit sphere, because all regions are cones. Do a BFS over regions from a seed by flipping facets (LP feasibility per facet). Only regions that contain part of a decision boundary or a misclassified part need Gaussian-mass computation: run GeoCert-style traversal along the decision boundary (2nd-largest index vs. network argmax). The result is a deterministic list of cones whose masses (item 1 / Genz) sum to the error. Prune with symmetry or rank-order structure: gates depend mostly on the record history.

## 4. Adaptive multilevel splitting for NN property violation (Webb, Rainforth, Teh & Kumar 2019) and generalized splitting (Botev & Kroese 2012)
- **Citation:** S. Webb, T. Rainforth, Y. W. Teh, M. P. Kumar, "A Statistical Approach to Assessing Neural Network Robustness," *ICLR 2019*, arXiv:1811.07209, code github.com/oval-group/statistical-robustness. Z. I. Botev, D. P. Kroese, "Efficient Monte Carlo simulation via the generalized splitting method," *Stat. Comput.* 22:1–16, 2012. F. Cérou, P. Del Moral, T. Furon, A. Guyader, "Sequential Monte Carlo for rare event estimation," *Stat. Comput.* 22:795–808, 2012.
- **Method:** Define a score s(x), e.g. the logit margin of the true 2nd-largest class. Estimate P(s(x) ≤ 0) as a product of conditional probabilities over adaptively chosen levels. Particles are moved by MCMC (Metropolis or pCN), which keeps N(0, I) invariant. The estimator has relative variance of roughly log(1/p)/N instead of 1/(pN).
- **Gaps:** (3).
- **Application here:** The error rate of about 4.7% is not tiny, so splitting alone gives a modest gain. Its value is per-stratum: inside strata where the error is rare (wide gaps between the top order statistics), use splitting or Botev tilting. Use crude or conditional MC where errors are common (near ties, or ≤1 positive input). The pCN move x' = ρx + √(1−ρ²)ξ works naturally with the Gaussian prior.

## 5. Genz–Bretz SOV algorithm and high-dimensional orthant SMC (Genz & Bretz 2009; Ridgway 2016; Azzimonti & Ginsbourger 2018)
- **Citation:** A. Genz, F. Bretz, *Computation of Multivariate Normal and t Probabilities*, Springer LNS 195, 2009 (just before the window, but the reference algorithm; code: R `mvtnorm::pmvnorm`, SciPy `multivariate_normal.cdf`). J. Ridgway, "Computation of Gaussian orthant probabilities in high dimension," *Stat. Comput.* 26:899–916, 2016. D. Azzimonti, D. Ginsbourger, "Estimating orthant probabilities of high-dimensional Gaussian vectors with an application to set estimation," *JCGS* 27(2):255–267, 2018 (R `anMC`).
- **Method:** Separation of variables: after a Cholesky reordering, write the probability as a nested integral over [0,1]^{d−1} of a smooth product of Φ-differences, then integrate with randomized lattice rules (QMC). Ridgway adds SMC for large d. Azzimonti–Ginsbourger split the dimensions into a cheap low-dimensional part computed by QMC and a remainder estimated by MC.
- **Gaps:** (1), (4).
- **Application here:** A cone {Ax ≥ 0} with k ≤ 10 linearly independent rows is an orthant probability for the k-dim Gaussian Ax ~ N(0, AAᵀ). Genz's pmvnorm reaches 1e-5 accuracy in milliseconds. For cones with more than 10 facets (typical for intersections of gate and decision constraints), either triangulate the cone into simplicial cones or use item 1, which handles more rows than dimensions. Simplicial cones also have closed forms in low dimension: the solid angle in 3D, and the Plackett/Childs reductions up to 4–5 dimensions.

## 6. Expectation propagation for Gaussian probabilities (Cunningham, Hennig & Lacoste-Julien 2011)
- **Citation:** J. P. Cunningham, P. Hennig, S. Lacoste-Julien, "Gaussian Probabilities and Expectation Propagation," arXiv:1111.6832, 2011 (also "Approximate Gaussian Integration using EP," 2012).
- **Method:** EP treats each linear constraint as a site factor and approximates the truncated Gaussian by a Gaussian. The normalizer gives P(polyhedron) deterministically in O(m d²) per sweep. It is very accurate for boxes and can be biased for general polyhedra.
- **Gaps:** (1), (5).
- **Application here:** A deterministic, near-instant estimate of each region's mass. Use it as a **control variate** or as the proposal mean and covariance for the importance sampler in items 1 and 4. Do not use it as the final answer, because of its bias on polyhedra.

## 7. Moment propagation through ReLU networks (Bibi, Alfadly & Ghanem 2018; Wu et al. 2019; Hernández-Lobato & Adams 2015; Gast & Roth 2018)
- **Citation:** A. Bibi, M. Alfadly, B. Ghanem, "Analytic Expressions for Probabilistic Moments of PL-DNN with Gaussian Input," *CVPR 2018*. A. Wu, S. Nowozin, E. Meeds, R. E. Turner, J. M. Hernández-Lobato, A. L. Gaunt, "Deterministic Variational Inference for Robust Bayesian Neural Networks," *ICLR 2019* (closed-form ReLU covariance propagation, code available). J. M. Hernández-Lobato, R. P. Adams, "Probabilistic Backpropagation," *ICML 2015*. J. Gast, S. Roth, "Lightweight Probabilistic Deep Networks," *CVPR 2018* (ADF through ReLU). Background: Cho & Saul 2009 arc-cosine kernels, which give E[ReLU(u)ReLU(v)] in closed form for jointly Gaussian (u, v).
- **Method:** Propagate mean and full covariance layer by layer. Each ReLU output's moments under a Gaussian input are closed-form (rectified Gaussian, arc-cosine kernel), and the output is re-approximated as Gaussian (ADF / moment matching).
- **Gaps:** (5).
- **Application here:** Unroll the 10 steps. Note the input x_t enters fresh at each step, so the pre-activation W_h h_t + W_i x_t needs the joint covariance of (h_t, x_{t:10}). Keep a 16+10-dim joint Gaussian approximation. The Gaussian approximation is crude for the argmax-of-logits accuracy, because h is far from Gaussian (sparse and conditioned on order statistics). Its value is as a **cheap control variate**: compute the Gaussian-approximation probability that the logit of class c wins (via Genz), then correct with MC or item 2 on the residual. A better variant conditions on the rank permutation (item 12) and applies ADF within each stratum, where gate patterns are nearly deterministic.

## 8. Exact HMC and elliptical slice sampling for linearly constrained Gaussians (Pakman & Paninski 2014; Murray, Adams & MacKay 2010)
- **Citation:** A. Pakman, L. Paninski, "Exact Hamiltonian Monte Carlo for Truncated Multivariate Gaussians," *JCGS* 23(2):518–542, 2014 (R `tmg`). I. Murray, R. P. Adams, D. J. C. MacKay, "Elliptical slice sampling," *AISTATS 2010*. Successor just outside the window: A. Gessner, O. Kanjilal, P. Hennig, "Integrals over Gaussians under Linear Domain Constraints" (LIN-ESS + Holmes–Diaconis–Ross), *AISTATS 2020*, code github.com/alpiges/LinConGauss.
- **Method:** For a Gaussian restricted to {Ax ≥ 0}, Hamiltonian trajectories are ellipses, and wall hits are found analytically, so the sampler is rejection-free. Elliptical slice sampling with analytic intersection of the ellipse and the half-spaces (LIN-ESS) also samples exactly within the domain. Paired with nested-domain HDR / subset simulation, this gives the integral.
- **Gaps:** (1), (3).
- **Application here:** These are the right MCMC kernels for item 4's splitting levels when the level sets are polyhedral. "Error region ∩ activation region" is polyhedral, and LIN-ESS ellipses intersect each half-space in closed form. Because the network is piecewise linear, an ellipse x(θ) = a cos θ + b sin θ through the whole network can be traced exactly by finding gate-flip angles. This gives an exact ellipse-slice sampler for the union of error cones without enumerating regions first.

## 9. Activation-pattern counts in practice (Hanin & Rolnick 2019)
- **Citation:** B. Hanin, D. Rolnick, "Complexity of Linear Regions in Deep Networks," *ICML 2019*, and "Deep ReLU Networks Have Surprisingly Few Activation Patterns," *NeurIPS 2019*.
- **Method:** Show that the expected number of regions crossing a line or a k-dim slice grows with the total neuron count, not exponentially with depth. They give formulas for the density of region boundaries.
- **Gaps:** (2).
- **Application here:** A feasibility check for item 3. With 160 neurons, a random line in R^10 should cross O(100) region boundaries, so the line walk in item 2 is cheap. The number of regions touching the sphere is likely 10^4–10^6, which is enumerable. Measure it empirically, using sampled or line-walked patterns, before investing in full enumeration.

## 10. Complete verification: Reluplex and MIP (Katz et al. 2017; Tjeng, Xiao & Tedrake 2019)
- **Citation:** G. Katz, C. Barrett, D. Dill, K. Julian, M. Kochenderfer, "Reluplex: An Efficient SMT Solver for Verifying Deep Neural Networks," *CAV 2017*. V. Tjeng, K. Xiao, R. Tedrake, "Evaluating Robustness of Neural Networks with Mixed Integer Programming," *ICLR 2019* (MIPVerify.jl). Also E. Wong, J. Z. Kolter, "Provable defenses via the convex outer adversarial polytope," *ICML 2018* and H. Zhang et al., "CROWN," *NeurIPS 2018* (cheap linear relaxation bounds).
- **Method:** Exact satisfiability or optimization over ReLU nets with big-M MILP or SMT and simplex-splitting. Bound-propagation methods (Wong–Kolter, CROWN) give fast sound bounds that prune most neurons as stably on or off within an input box or cone.
- **Gaps:** (2).
- **Application here:** Certify whole strata as "always correct". Example: the stratum where the gap between the 2nd and 3rd order statistics is at least δ and other conditions hold. Certifying is an LP/MILP over a polyhedron in x after fixing the rank order, which is itself a polyhedral cone (a Weyl chamber). Certified strata contribute their exact Gaussian measure, which is known analytically from order statistics. MC or the other methods are then spent only on the uncertified remainder. This is stratification with zero variance on certified strata.

## 11. Multilevel Monte Carlo and control variates (Giles 2015)
- **Citation:** M. B. Giles, "Multilevel Monte Carlo methods," *Acta Numerica* 24:259–328, 2015 (original: *Operations Research* 2008). See also Giles, Nagapetyan & Ritter, "Multilevel Monte Carlo approximation of distribution functions and densities," *SIAM/ASA JUQ* 2015, which treats indicator (discontinuous) payoffs via smoothing.
- **Method:** Write E[f] = E[f_0] + Σ E[f_l − f_{l−1}] with cheap, correlated approximations f_l, and spend more samples on the cheap levels. For discontinuous f, smooth the indicator or use conditional expectations (as in item 2) to recover good variance decay.
- **Gaps:** (4).
- **Application here:** Possible levels: f_0 = an analytic surrogate (e.g. "network outputs the true 2nd-largest index unless an order-statistic gap is small", computed exactly from Gaussian order statistics), f_1 = the network with gates frozen to their rank-order-predicted pattern, which is linear per chamber and has exact cone masses, and f_2 = the true network. The coupled differences f_2 − f_1 are nonzero only where the gate prediction fails, which is rare, so their variance is small. This is effectively a two-level control-variate scheme.

## 12. Gaussian order statistics and Weyl-chamber stratification
- **Citation:** R. B. Arellano-Valle, M. G. Genton, "On the exact distribution of the maximum of absolutely continuous dependent random variables," *Stat. Prob. Lett.* 78:27–35, 2008 (borderline date). Standard reference: H. A. David, H. N. Nagaraja, *Order Statistics*, 3rd ed., 2003. For record-time statistics see B. C. Arnold, N. Balakrishnan, H. N. Nagaraja, *Records*, Wiley 1998.
- **Method:** For i.i.d. N(0,1), the joint density of order statistics and spacings is explicit, and all 10! rank permutations are equally likely. Spacing distributions near the top, e.g. P(X_(9) − X_(8) < ε), have simple 1D integral forms.
- **Gaps:** (6), (4) through stratification.
- **Application here:** (a) Stratify by rank permutation π, or by the coarser record history the gates depend on. Every stratum has known probability 1/10! × multiplicity, so stratified sampling removes the between-stratum variance. (b) Within a stratum, x = Pσ for sorted σ, and σ has the explicit order-statistic density. Error regions are thin bands in the top spacings (σ_1 − σ_2, σ_2 − σ_3), so use 1D exact integration over these spacings, which is preintegration from item 2 in the most natural coordinate. (c) The "≤1 positive input" region has exact probability (1 + 10)/2^10 ≈ 1.07%, by symmetry and independence of signs. If the network fails on a known fraction of it, that fraction can be computed per sign pattern.

## Suggested pipeline (combining the top items)
1. Stratify by sign pattern and rank or record structure (item 12), or certify easy strata with LP/bound propagation (item 10).
2. In the uncertain strata, use the conditional line or great-circle estimator with exact breakpoint walking (item 2), driven by scrambled Sobol' points. Its variance should be far below crude MC at equal cost, because each sample integrates a whole 1D slice exactly.
3. For a deterministic answer, enumerate error-touching regions by facet traversal (item 3) and sum their cone masses with Botev tilting or Genz SOV (items 1 and 5). Use EP or moment propagation (items 6 and 7) as control variates.
