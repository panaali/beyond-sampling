# Literature 2000–2009: deductive accuracy estimation for a piecewise-linear ReLU RNN

Problem recap: accuracy = Gaussian measure (equivalently, normalized solid angle on S^9) of a finite union of
polyhedral cones in R^10, one cone per (gate pattern, argmax-correct) region. Errors live in thin wedges near ties
of top order statistics, plus the "at most 1 positive input" region. We want an estimator with lower MSE than plain MC at
equal compute, over weight perturbations.

Verification status: [V] = citation details checked via web search in this session; [M] = from memory, details
(volume and page numbers) should be double-checked.

## Ranked items

### 1. Line sampling / conditional (Rao-Blackwellized) MC along lines and great circles  [gaps 3, 4]
- **Koutsourelakis, P.S., Pradlwarter, H.J., Schuëller, G.I. (2004).** "Reliability of structures in high dimensions, part I: algorithms and applications." *Probabilistic Engineering Mechanics* 19(4):409–417. doi:10.1016/j.probengmech.2004.05.001. Companion: Koutsourelakis (2004), Part II: theoretical validation, same issue, 419–423. Also Schuëller, Pradlwarter, Koutsourelakis (2004), "A critical appraisal of reliability estimation procedures for high dimensions," same issue, 463–474. [V]
- **Method:** Pick an "important direction" u. Write x = t·u + z with z ⟂ u. For each sampled z, find the failure interval(s) in t along the line exactly and integrate the 1-D Gaussian analytically. This is conditional MC, so variance is never above plain MC (Rao–Blackwell). It helps most when the failure boundary is nearly flat across u.
- **Application:** Along any line the network is piecewise linear in t. A sweep can find every gate breakpoint exactly: track the pre-activations, which are affine in t within each pattern, and find the next zero crossing over the 10 steps. Each sampled line then gives an exact 1-D Gaussian measure of the "correct" t-set instead of a 0/1 indicator. **Homogeneity variant (stronger):** sample a uniformly random 2-plane through the origin. The isotropic Gaussian restricted to that plane has a uniform angle, so the arc-length fraction of the great circle inside the correct set is an unbiased estimate of accuracy. That fraction is exactly computable: each pre-activation is a·cosθ + b·sinθ, so crossings are closed form. Pair it with RQMC over the plane orientations (item 6). The tie bands become short arcs that are measured exactly rather than hit or missed. This is the most direct path to beating MC.

### 2. Genz & Bretz: methods and software for MVN probabilities over polyhedra  [gap 1]
- **Genz, A., Bretz, F. (2009).** *Computation of Multivariate Normal and t Probabilities.* Lecture Notes in Statistics 195, Springer. Also **Genz & Bretz (2002)**, "Comparison of methods for the computation of multivariate t probabilities," *J. Comput. Graph. Stat.* 11(4):950–971; **Genz (2004)**, "Numerical computation of rectangular bivariate and trivariate normal and t probabilities," *Statistics and Computing* 14:251–260. [M]
- **Method:** Separation-of-variables (SOV) transform. After a Cholesky factorization and variable reordering, P(a ≤ Lz ≤ b) becomes an integral over [0,1]^{d-1} of a smooth product of conditional normal CDF widths. The integrand is then evaluated with randomized lattice rules (QMC). Errors for d ≤ 10 are typically 1e-5 to 1e-6 with about 1e4 points. The book also covers general linear-inequality regions {Ax ≤ b} with more rows than dimensions, by reduction to singular MVN.
- **Application:** Each gate-pattern region is a cone {x : A_k x ≥ 0} in R^10. When the number of active facets is ≤ 10 (simplicial cone), its Gaussian measure is an MVN orthant probability of A_k x, available in mvtnorm `pmvnorm` to high accuracy. Strategy: enumerate the regions that carry most of the mass (rank-order-defined) or, better, the *error* regions (thin wedges), compute each exactly via SOV-QMC, and sum. Non-simplicial cones need triangulation (item 7) or an inclusion–exclusion of simplicial pieces.

### 3. Miwa–Hayter–Kuriki recursive orthant algorithm (and Hayter's recursive integration)  [gaps 1, 6]
- **Miwa, T., Hayter, A.J., Kuriki, S. (2003).** "The evaluation of general non-centred orthant probabilities." *JRSS B* 65(1):223–234. doi:10.1111/1467-9868.00382. Implemented as `Miwa()` in R mvtnorm (d < 20). [V]
- **Hayter, A.J. (2006).** "Recursive integration methodologies with statistical applications." *J. Statist. Plann. Inference* 136(7):2284–2296. [V title; volume and pages M]
- **Method:** Any m-dim orthant probability is written as a signed sum of at most (m−1)! "orthoscheme" probabilities. An orthoscheme has tridiagonal (Markov) structure, so it is computed by recursive 1-D integration on a grid: a chain of convolutions of the form ∫Φ·φ. This is deterministic, near-exact for d ≤ ~10, and has no sampling noise.
- **Application:** This fits the ranking events naturally. "Input i is 2nd largest" decomposes into 9 orthant events in the differences x_j − x_i, each a Markov-chain-like ordering. The network's gate history ("record history" of the sequence) is also a chain of order relations. For each dominant cone, Miwa gives a deterministic value with error control. It is the right tool to get the *baseline* mass of the 10·9 = 90 "top-2 order" cones and the correction terms near ties. It also produces exact ranking probabilities for sanity checks (each is 1/10 by symmetry, but conditional ones are not).

### 4. Craig's geometric reconstruction of orthant probabilities  [gaps 1, 6]
- **Craig, P. (2008).** "A new reconstruction of multivariate normal orthant probabilities." *JRSS B* 70(1):227–243. doi:10.1111/j.1467-9868.2007.00625.x. Software (R packages `orthant`, `fftplus`): https://www.maths.dur.ac.uk/users/p.s.craig/orthants [V]
- **Method:** Geometric decomposition rewrites any non-singular orthant probability in terms of orthant probabilities of autoregressive (banded) sequences. Those are evaluated by FFT-based recursive convolution, which is fast and accurate in moderate dimension.
- **Application:** This is an alternative or cross-check to item 3 for computing the measure of each cone exactly. It is especially fast when the constraint matrix is close to banded, which is what happens when a cone is defined by successive comparisons x_{t} vs. running max along the time axis.

### 5. Solid-angle series for simplicial cones (Aomoto / Ribando)  [gap 1]
- **Ribando, J.M. (2006).** "Measuring solid angles beyond dimension three." *Discrete & Computational Geometry* 36(3):479–487. doi:10.1007/s00454-006-1253-4. [V]
- **Method:** The normalized solid angle of a simplicial cone spanned by unit vectors v_1..v_n is given by an explicit multivariate Taylor series in the off-diagonal inner products α_ij = v_i·v_j, with closed-form coefficients. It converges when the associated matrix is positive definite. Truncation gives analytic approximations, and analytic derivatives with respect to the generators (hence the weights) come for free.
- **Application:** Our accuracy is literally a sum of solid angles of cones. For perturbation studies over random weight copies, the series gives a *smooth, differentiable* map from the weights to each cone's angle. One can compute accuracy at the nominal weights once (expensively) and then propagate perturbations to first or second order. That could beat MC MSE because the perturbation-induced change is evaluated deterministically. A caveat: it needs decomposition into simplicial cones, and convergence fails for very obtuse cones. Later work (Fitisone & Zhou 2023) fixes this, but it falls outside the date range.

### 6. Randomized QMC for Markov chains (array-RQMC) and RQMC for discontinuous integrands  [gap 4]
- **L'Ecuyer, P., Lécot, C., Tuffin, B. (2008).** "A randomized quasi-Monte Carlo simulation method for Markov chains." *Operations Research* 56(4):958–975. doi:10.1287/opre.1080.0556. [V]
- **L'Ecuyer, P., Lemieux, C. (2002).** "Recent advances in randomized quasi-Monte Carlo methods." In *Modeling Uncertainty*, Kluwer, 419–474. [M]
- **Owen, A.B. (2008).** "Local antithetic sampling with scrambled nets." *Annals of Statistics* 36(5):2319–2343. [M]
- **Method:** Array-RQMC simulates n chain copies in parallel. At each step it sorts the states and drives them with a fresh randomized low-discrepancy point set, which gives variance reductions up to 1000x in reported cases. Owen and L'Ecuyer–Lemieux explain why scrambled nets still beat MC (about n^{-1-1/(2d)}-type rates) when the integrand has discontinuities on hyperplanes. The gain grows when one first smooths the integrand.
- **Application:** The RNN *is* a 10-step Markov chain: state h_t ∈ R^16, with a fresh N(0,1)·column input each step. Array-RQMC applies directly: sort the hidden states (e.g., by a 1-D projection such as the current logit margin) and use 2-D RQMC points per step (state rank, x_t). Simpler option: Sobol/lattice RQMC on the plain 10-D integrand. Because the indicator has only hyperplane-type discontinuities, rates beat n^{-1/2}, and combining with item 1's line/plane smoothing (to remove the discontinuity) should be close to the n^{-3/2} regime.

### 7. Conditioning / one-step survival smoothing (Glasserman & Staum; Glasserman's book)  [gaps 3, 4]
- **Glasserman, P., Staum, J. (2001).** "Conditioning on one-step survival for barrier option simulations." *Operations Research* 49(6):923–937. doi:10.1287/opre.49.6.923.10018. [V]
- **Glasserman, P. (2004).** *Monte Carlo Methods in Financial Engineering.* Springer (chs. 4.1–4.6: control variates, conditional MC, stratification, importance sampling; ch. 5: QMC). [M]
- **Method:** At each time step, sample the next Gaussian increment *conditional on staying in the "alive" region* (a truncated normal) and multiply the estimator by the survival probability. The terminal indicator becomes a smooth product of conditional probabilities. This is a sequential conditional MC that removes discontinuities.
- **Application:** Process inputs sequentially. At step t, given h_t, the set of x_t values that keep the "eventual correct answer" feasible is not available in closed form. However, the gate switches of h_{t+1} are hyperplanes in x_t (a scalar input times a column of W_i plus a fixed vector), so x_t ∈ R is cut into intervals with known Gaussian mass. One can stratify x_t across those intervals: sample a gate interval proportionally to its mass, then x_t within it. The last step t = 10 is exactly computable. For given h_9 and the earlier inputs, the correct-label set in x_10 (and the "2nd largest" condition, which depends on x_10 through order relations) is a finite union of intervals. Integrating the last coordinate analytically is a guaranteed variance reduction at small cost. Glasserman's book also provides control-variate ideas: use a surrogate "ideal sorter" whose accuracy is known (100% or analytic) and regress.

### 8. Subset simulation and MCMC for small-probability failure regions  [gap 3]
- **Au, S.K., Beck, J.L. (2001).** "Estimation of small failure probabilities in high dimensions by subset simulation." *Probabilistic Engineering Mechanics* 16(4):263–277. [V]
- **Method:** Write a small failure probability as a product of larger conditional probabilities over nested events F_1 ⊃ F_2 ⊃ … defined by thresholds of a continuous performance function. Each level is estimated by a component-wise (modified) Metropolis chain seeded from the previous level's failure samples.
- **Application:** The error rate is 4.7%, which is not tiny, so subset simulation is not a big win as a whole-estimator. Use it to *map* the error regions instead. Take the performance function g(x) = logit_correct − max_other logit, which is continuous and homogeneous of degree 1, so work on the sphere g(x)/|x|. MCMC samples from the error set then reveal which cones (gate patterns and order patterns) make up the error. Those cones can be enumerated and integrated exactly with items 2–5, and MC is kept only for the residual.

### 9. Directional simulation (Ditlevsen tradition; Nie & Ellingwood 2000)  [gap 3]
- **Nie, J., Ellingwood, B.R. (2000).** "Directional methods for structural reliability analysis." *Structural Safety* 22(3):233–249. Also Nie & Ellingwood (2005), *J. Eng. Mech.* 131(3):259–267: directional simulation with deterministic point sets on the sphere. [M; the 2005 paper is V]
- **Method:** Sample directions on S^{d-1}. Along each ray, the failure probability is a chi-distribution tail beyond the limit-state radius. With deterministic, uniformly spread direction sets (spherical t-designs or QMC on the sphere), the error drops below MC.
- **Application:** Because the network is positively homogeneous, every ray is entirely correct or entirely wrong, so the radial part is trivial. Directional simulation reduces to "sphere-fraction estimation," and the useful content is the *deterministic spherical point sets* (QMC on S^9) and their stratification. The great-circle exact-arc estimator of item 1 is the natural refinement, since it replaces each direction with a 1-D family integrated exactly. Combine with spherical stratification by the rank-order chamber: there are 10! Weyl chambers, each of exactly equal mass 1/10!, so this is free stratification with known weights. Then sample within a chamber.

### 10. Multi-parametric programming / explicit MPC region enumeration  [gap 2]
- **Tøndel, P., Johansen, T.A., Bemporad, A. (2003).** "An algorithm for multi-parametric quadratic programming and explicit MPC solutions." *Automatica* 39(3):489–497. Conference version: IEEE CDC 2001. [V title; volume and pages M]
- **Bemporad, A., Ferrari-Trecate, G., Morari, M. (2000).** "Observability and controllability of piecewise affine and hybrid systems." *IEEE Trans. Automatic Control* 45(10):1864–1876. [M]
- **Kvasnica, M., Grieder, P., Baotić, M. (2004).** Multi-Parametric Toolbox (MPT). [M]
- **Method:** Enumerate the polyhedral partition of a parameter space on which a piecewise-affine map has fixed active set. Start from one region, find its facets with LPs, step across each facet to the neighbor, and repeat. This is a graph search over adjacent regions with LP-based redundancy removal.
- **Application:** This is the algorithm for exactly enumerating the linear regions (gate patterns) of the RNN on the sphere. Each region is a cone with an LP-checkable facet description. Restrict enumeration to regions that intersect the *decision boundary*, i.e., cones where the argmax changes, and then compute only the error-cone masses (items 2–5). Accuracy = 1 − Σ(error cone measures). The 2000 Bemporad et al. paper gives the MLD/PWA framing for treating h_{t+1} = ReLU(·) as a switched linear system with mode-dependent guards. Expect the number of regions to be the bottleneck, but the "gates mostly determined by rank order" observation suggests the effective count is manageable.

### 11. Zonotope / polyhedral reachability for piecewise-affine dynamics  [gap 2]
- **Girard, A. (2005).** "Reachability of uncertain linear systems using zonotopes." *HSCC 2005*, LNCS 3414:291–305. [M; widely cited, details not directly fetched]
- **Asarin, E., Dang, T., Girard, A. (2003/2007)**, hybridization methods for nonlinear reachability. [M]
- **Method:** Propagate sets (zonotopes = affine images of cubes) through linear maps exactly and through switching via over-approximation. The cost is polynomial in dimension.
- **Application:** Propagate the cone or region through time symbolically. Sets of inputs whose gate status at step t is *certain* (always on or always off) are proved via zonotope bounds on pre-activations over a region (e.g., over a rank-order chamber intersected with a sphere cap). This certifies large chunks of input space as definitely correct, with their mass computed exactly via orthant formulas. The uncertain remainder (thin tie bands) is passed to items 1, 2 and 7. This is a branch-and-bound "certify, then integrate" scheme.

### 12. Expectation propagation / assumed density filtering for truncated Gaussians  [gap 5]
- **Minka, T.P. (2001).** "Expectation propagation for approximate Bayesian inference." *UAI 2001*; and Minka's PhD thesis (MIT, 2001), "A family of algorithms for approximate Bayesian inference." [M]
- **Opper, M., Winther, O. (2005).** "Expectation consistent approximate inference." *JMLR* 6:2177–2204. [M]
- **Julier, S.J., Uhlmann, J.K. (2004).** "Unscented filtering and nonlinear estimation." *Proc. IEEE* 92(3):401–422. [M]
- **Method:** ADF/EP approximates a Gaussian prior multiplied by many step-function (half-space) factors by a single Gaussian matched in moments, one factor at a time. EP iterates the matching to self-consistency. The normalizer gives an approximation to the polytope probability, and in moderate dimension it is often accurate to about 1e-3 relative error. (Cunningham, Hennig & Lacoste-Julien later formalized "Gaussian probabilities and EP" in 2011, just outside the range.)
- **Application:** (a) Approximate each cone's mass by the EP normalizer of N(0,I)·Π 1[a_k·x ≥ 0]: this is fast and deterministic, though its bias needs checking against item 2. (b) For moment propagation through the RNN, the ReLU of a Gaussian has closed-form mean and variance (rectified Gaussian), so ADF through 10 steps gives a Gaussian approximation of the logits. However, the logits are *not* close to Gaussian because of the mixture over gate patterns, so a plain single-Gaussian pass will be biased. A Gaussian-sum version (one component per dominant gate pattern / order class) is the reasonable middle ground. Its likely best use is as a *control variate* for MC/RQMC rather than as a stand-alone estimator.

### 13. Exact polytope volume (triangulation / Lawrence) and lattice-point tools  [gap 1]
- **Büeler, B., Enge, A., Fukuda, K. (2000).** "Exact volume computation for polytopes: a practical study." In *Polytopes – Combinatorics and Computation*, DMV Seminar 29, Birkhäuser, 131–154. doi:10.1007/978-3-0348-8438-9_6. Software: `vinci`. [V]
- **Method:** Compares triangulation (Delaunay, boundary), Lawrence's signed decomposition, and Lasserre's recursive formula for exact volumes. It finds triangulation practical up to roughly d = 10–15 with moderately many facets.
- **Application:** Mostly useful for the *decomposition* step: triangulate a non-simplicial cone (or its intersection with a hyperplane x·1 = 1 or a chamber) into simplicial cones, each of which is a single orthant probability (items 2–4) or solid angle (item 5). Use Lebesgue volume only as a sanity check, since we need Gaussian measure, not volume. Gaussian measure of a cone equals its normalized solid angle, which in turn equals the volume of the cone ∩ unit ball divided by the ball volume. That makes exact volume of cone ∩ cross-polytope or simplex slices usable for degenerate cases.

### 14. Gaussian ranking / order-statistic probabilities via equicorrelated orthants  [gap 6]
- Background (classic, pre-2000): Steck & Owen (1962) and Dunnett & Sobel give the one-dimensional integral representation of equicorrelated (ρ = 1/2) orthant probabilities for "x_i exceeds x_j for all j in S". The 2000s computational treatment is in Genz & Bretz (2009, ch. 2–4) and Miwa et al. (2003). Hayter & Liu's 2000s work on multiple comparisons of ordered means uses the same recursive structure. [M]
- **Method:** Events like {x_i is 2nd largest} ∩ {specific earlier/later relations} reduce to orthant probabilities of contrast vectors. These have structured correlation (one-factor or Markov), so they collapse to a 1-D or 2-D integral: condition on the common factor (for one-factor structure, condition on x_i) and use a product of Φ's.
- **Application:** The "bands near ties among top order statistics" have a closed-form description. Condition on (x_(1), x_(2), x_(3)) and the gap sizes; the remaining 7 inputs are i.i.d. truncated normals below x_(3). If the network's correctness depends only on a low-dimensional statistic of the top few values plus aggregate stats of the rest (which the observed gate structure suggests), accuracy reduces to a 3–4-dimensional integral over the joint density of top order statistics (n!·Πφ·Φ^{n−k}), done by product Gauss–Hermite or Genz cubature. This is the most "deductive" route and can be checked against items 2–3 on individual cones.

## Suggested pipeline (synthesis)
1. **Free stratification:** the 10!/(10·9) symmetry classes and rank-order chambers have known equal masses. Condition on the identity of the top-2 positions and the order of their arrival times.
2. **Certify and enumerate:** use region exploration (item 10) plus zonotope certificates (item 11), seeded by subset-simulation samples from the error set (item 8), to list error cones. Compute their masses deterministically with Genz SOV-QMC, Miwa, or Craig (items 2–4), after triangulation (item 13) if needed.
3. **Residual by smoothed RQMC:** for whatever is not enumerated, use the exact great-circle/line estimator (item 1). Drive the plane orientations with scrambled Sobol (item 6), with last-step analytic conditioning (item 7), and use the EP/ADF approximation as a control variate (item 12).
4. **Perturbation studies:** differentiate cone solid angles with respect to the weights (item 5) to reuse nominal computations across perturbed copies.
