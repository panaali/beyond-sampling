# Pre-2000 literature for deductive accuracy estimation of a ReLU RNN

Problem recap: accuracy = Gaussian measure (= normalized solid angle on S^9) of a union of
polyhedral cones in R^10, one cone per activation pattern (gate sequence over 10 steps x 16 units),
intersected with the "logit of 2nd-largest position wins" half-spaces. Errors live in thin wedges
near ties among the top order statistics and in a low-positive-count region.

The single most useful reframing from this literature: **stop expanding around a typical input and
instead (a) enumerate the dominant cones, (b) compute each cone's Gaussian measure with a
near-exact orthant-probability method, and (c) treat the thin error bands as boundary/tube
contributions computed analytically.** Each piece has a classic, well-tested method.

Citation details marked (verified) were checked against publisher/index pages; others are from
standard references and should be double-checked before citing formally.

---

## Ranked items

### 1. Genz (1992) — sequential conditioning transform for MVN rectangle/cone probabilities
**Citation.** A. Genz, "Numerical computation of multivariate normal probabilities,"
*J. Computational and Graphical Statistics* 1(2):141–149, 1992. (verified)
Related, same idea in econometrics: Geweke (1989/1991), Hajivassiliou & McFadden, Keane (1994,
*Econometrica* 62:95–116) — the "GHK simulator".

**Method.** Write P(Ax ≥ 0), x ~ N(0, I), via Cholesky of A Aᵀ; transform successively so each
coordinate is drawn from its *truncated* conditional, mapping the problem to an integral over
[0,1]^{d-1} of a smooth product of Φ-differences. The integrand has low variance and is smooth, so
it works extremely well with lattice/QMC rules (item 7). Error typically 1e-4 to 1e-6 in d≈10 with
a few thousand points.

**Gaps.** (1), (4).

**Application.** For a fixed gate pattern the network is linear, so the set "correct and in this
pattern" is a polyhedral cone {x : Gx ≥ 0} with G's rows = gate constraints over 10 steps plus the
9 "correct logit wins" constraints. Remove redundant constraints by LP (most of the ~160 gate
inequalities are implied by a handful of rank-order constraints), then Genz/GHK on the irreducible
facets. Because the cone is defined by *ranks* mostly, many cones are Weyl-chamber-like
(x_{σ1} ≥ x_{σ2} ≥ …) with a few extra cuts — exactly the regime where Genz is near-exact. This
replaces MC hit-or-miss (variance p(1-p)) with a conditional estimator whose variance is orders of
magnitude smaller, directly beating the MC-at-equal-compute bar.

### 2. Hotelling (1939) / Weyl (1939) / Naiman (1990) / Johansen & Johnstone (1990) — tube formulas for spherical polyhedra
**Citations.**
- H. Hotelling, "Tubes and spheres in n-spaces, and a class of statistical problems,"
  *Amer. J. Math.* 61:440–460, 1939.
- H. Weyl, "On the volume of tubes," *Amer. J. Math.* 61:461–472, 1939.
- D. Q. Naiman, "Volumes of tubular neighborhoods of spherical polyhedra and statistical
  inference," *Ann. Statist.* 18(2):685–716, 1990. (verified)
- S. Johansen & I. M. Johnstone, "Hotelling's theorem on the volume of tubes: some illustrations in
  simultaneous inference and data analysis," *Ann. Statist.* 18(2):652–684, 1990. (verified)
- J. Sun, "Tail probabilities of the maxima of Gaussian random fields," *Ann. Probab.* 21:34–71, 1993.

**Method.** The spherical volume of an ε-neighborhood of a piecewise-smooth set M ⊂ S^{d-1} is a
polynomial in sin ε / cos ε with coefficients given by curvature integrals (Lipschitz–Killing
curvatures); for spherical polyhedra these reduce to sums over faces of (face volume) × (external
angle). Naiman gives the version for spherical polytopes and bounds that are exact to leading
order.

**Gaps.** (1), (2).

**Application.** The error set is (mostly) a union of thin wedges around the tie hyperplanes
x_(1)=x_(2), x_(2)=x_(3) restricted to the relevant cones. If the network's decision boundary near
a tie is a tilted hyperplane at small angle θ from the true tie hyperplane, the misclassified wedge
has measure ≈ (θ/π) × (measure of the tie face, a (d−2)-dimensional spherical polytope) plus a
corner correction from codimension-2 faces (external-angle terms). That gives a closed-form,
*first-order-exact-in-θ* error estimate per facet — the boundary-layer analogue of what the current
perturbation expansion is trying to approximate from the interior. Face measures themselves are
lower-dimensional orthant probabilities (item 1/3).

### 3. Plackett (1954) reduction formula + Kendall/Pearson tetrachoric series
**Citations.**
- R. L. Plackett, "A reduction formula for normal multivariate integrals," *Biometrika*
  41(3-4):351–360, 1954. (verified)
- M. G. Kendall, "Proof of relations connected with the tetrachoric series and its
  generalization," *Biometrika* 32:196–198, 1941.
- G. P. Steck, "A table for computing trivariate normal probabilities," *Ann. Math. Statist.*
  29:780–800, 1958; and Steck, "Orthant probabilities for four-variate normal…," *Ann. Math.
  Statist.* 33, 1962.
- S. S. Gupta, "Probability integrals of multivariate normal and multivariate t," *Ann. Math.
  Statist.* 34:792–828, 1963 (survey + bibliography).
- I. G. Abrahamson, "Orthant probabilities for the quadrivariate normal distribution," *Ann. Math.
  Statist.* 35:1685–1703, 1964.
- R. M. Bacon (1963) / Childs (1967, *Biometrika* 54:293–299) for 4-D reductions.

**Method.** Plackett: ∂P(orthant)/∂ρ_ij = φ₂(0,0;ρ_ij) × P(lower-dim orthant | x_i=x_j=0). So an
orthant probability can be obtained by integrating along a path in correlation space from a
matrix where the answer is known (identity, or equicorrelated) to the target. Kendall's tetrachoric
series expands the orthant probability as a power series in the off-diagonal correlations — a
literal perturbation/diagrammatic expansion (each term is a product of Hermite moments, i.e. a sum
over graphs on the variables). Schläfli's formula is the same statement in solid-angle language:
d(vol) = Σ (codim-2 face volume) dθ_face / (d−2).

**Gaps.** (1), (5).

**Application.** This is the principled version of "expand around a typical case". Choose the
reference cone as the exact Weyl chamber {x_{σ1} ≥ … } (measure 1/10! by symmetry, or more usefully
the "true answer" region whose measure is exactly 1 by construction); the network's actual
decision cone differs by small tilts of a few facets. Plackett/Schläfli give the first-order change
in measure as Σ_facets (codim-2 face measure) × (dihedral-angle change) — the dihedral angles are
computable directly from W_o, W_h, W_i products. Second order uses the derivative again. Unlike a
pointwise Taylor expansion of the network, this expansion is in *angles of the cone*, which is
exactly where the measure is sensitive, so it should converge far faster.

### 4. Breitung (1984), Hohenbichler & Rackwitz (1983), Hasofer & Lind (1974) — FORM/SORM from structural reliability
**Citations.**
- K. Breitung, "Asymptotic approximations for multinormal integrals," *J. Eng. Mech. (ASCE)*
  110(3):357–366, 1984. (verified)
- M. Hohenbichler & R. Rackwitz, "First-order concepts in system reliability," *Structural Safety*
  1(3):177–188, 1983. (verified)
- A. M. Hasofer & N. C. Lind, "Exact and invariant second-moment code format," *J. Eng. Mech. Div.
  ASCE* 100:111–121, 1974.
- K. Breitung & M. Hohenbichler, "Asymptotic approximations for multivariate integrals with an
  application to multinormal probabilities," *J. Multivariate Anal.* 30:80–97, 1989.
- Ditlevsen (1979) "Narrow reliability bounds for structural systems," *J. Struct. Mech.* 7:453–472.

**Method.** Failure probability P(g(x) ≤ 0) for x ~ N(0,I) is dominated by "design points" — points
on the boundary closest to the origin. FORM linearizes g there (P ≈ Φ(−β)); SORM adds a curvature
correction Π(1+βκ_i)^{-1/2} (Breitung). System reliability (unions/intersections of failure modes)
is handled by multinormal probabilities of the linearized modes (Hohenbichler–Rackwitz) with
Ditlevsen bounds for unions.

**Gaps.** (2), (4).

**Application.** Our network is homogeneous, so "distance from origin" is not the right scale —
but restricted to the sphere (or by conditioning on the radius, chi distribution with 10 dof) the
same machinery applies: each error mode is a polyhedral region whose boundary is piecewise linear
(SORM curvature = 0 inside a cone; kinks contribute as system intersections). The FORM/system
reliability viewpoint gives a recipe: list failure modes (each "wrong class k wins under pattern P"),
linearize, then compute union probability with Ditlevsen bounds or the exact MVN of item 1. Also
suggests **importance sampling centered at design points** (Rackwitz/Bucher-type IS) as a variance
reducer if a sampling hybrid is allowed.

### 5. Bechhofer (1954), Dunnett & Sobel (1955), Gupta (1956/1963) — probability of correct selection / equicorrelated reductions
**Citations.**
- R. E. Bechhofer, "A single-sample multiple decision procedure for ranking means of normal
  populations with known variances," *Ann. Math. Statist.* 25:16–39, 1954.
- C. W. Dunnett & M. Sobel, "Approximations to the probability integral and certain percentage
  points of a multivariate analogue of Student's t-distribution," *Biometrika* 42:258–260, 1955.
- S. S. Gupta (1963), above; Gupta, Nagel & Panchapakesan, "On the order statistics from equally
  correlated normal random variables," *Biometrika* 60:403–413, 1973.
- Y. L. Tong, *The Multivariate Normal Distribution*, Springer 1990 (Ch. 8: one-dimensional
  integral representations).

**Method.** For equicorrelated (or "one-factor": Σ = D + λλᵀ) covariance, the orthant probability
reduces to a *one-dimensional* integral: P = ∫ φ(z) Π_i Φ((a_i + λ_i z)/√d_i) dz. Ranking-and-
selection theory computes P(correct selection) = P(the right coordinate is the max), which is
structurally our "2nd-largest" event.

**Gaps.** (1), (3).

**Application.** The tie constraints x_i − x_j have rank-one-plus-diagonal covariance structure;
"x_k is the 2nd largest" = ∪_j {x_j > x_k > all others}, each a cone whose covariance after the
change of variables y_i = x_k − x_i is of factor form, so its measure is a 1-D integral (Gauss–
Hermite, ~20 nodes, machine precision). If the network's decision cones can be approximated as
Weyl-chamber cones with small facet tilts, one can (a) compute the base cone in 1-D exactly and
(b) apply item 3 for the tilts. Also give the exact closed form for the baseline "ideal algorithm"
accuracy restricted to the low-positive-count region.

### 6. Rényi (1953) representation and normal-spacing results (David, *Order Statistics*)
**Citations.**
- A. Rényi, "On the theory of order statistics," *Acta Math. Acad. Sci. Hungar.* 4:191–231, 1953.
- H. A. David, *Order Statistics*, Wiley, 1970 (2nd ed. 1981).
- H. Ruben, "On the moments of order statistics in samples from normal populations," *Biometrika*
  41:200–227, 1954 (moments as solid angles / volumes of spherical simplices; see also Ruben 1960,
  1961). (citation verified, geometric content partly via later papers)
- R. Pyke, "Spacings," *J. R. Statist. Soc. B* 27:395–449, 1965.
- H. L. Harter, "Expected values of normal order statistics," *Biometrika* 48:151–165, 1961 (tables).

**Method.** Rényi: exponential order statistics are partial sums of independent scaled
exponentials; for general F, X_(k) = F^{-1}(1 − exp(−Σ E_j/(n−j+1))). Spacings of normal order
statistics near the top are approximately independent with densities computable from φ/Φ and the
hazard. Ruben shows normal order-statistic moments are solid angles of simplicial cones — the same
objects as our accuracy.

**Gaps.** (3), (2).

**Application.** Change coordinates to (rank permutation σ, top value u = x_(1), spacings
s_1 = x_(1)−x_(2), s_2 = x_(2)−x_(3), …). The error bands are {s_1 small} and {s_2 small}; the
joint density of (s_1, s_2) at 0 is computable exactly: f_{s1,s2}(0,0) = n(n−1)(n−2)∫φ(u)³Φ(u)^{n−3} du.
Combined with item 2, the misclassified measure ≈ Σ_bands (density of spacing at 0) × (effective
band width), where the band width is read off the network's logit gaps as a linear function of the
spacing. This is the "boundary-layer" leading-order term and is deterministic.

### 7. Quasi-Monte Carlo and lattice rules: Korobov (1959), Hlawka (1962), Halton (1960), Sobol' (1967), Niederreiter (1992), Owen (1997), Hammersley & Handscomb (1964)
**Citations.**
- N. M. Korobov, "The approximate computation of multiple integrals," *Dokl. Akad. Nauk SSSR*
  124:1207–1210, 1959.
- J. H. Halton, "On the efficiency of certain quasi-random sequences of points in evaluating
  multi-dimensional integrals," *Numer. Math.* 2:84–90, 1960.
- I. M. Sobol', "On the distribution of points in a cube and the approximate evaluation of
  integrals," *USSR Comput. Math. Math. Phys.* 7(4):86–112, 1967.
- J. M. Hammersley & D. C. Handscomb, *Monte Carlo Methods*, Methuen, 1964 (antithetic, control
  variates, conditional MC, stratification).
- H. Niederreiter, *Random Number Generation and Quasi-Monte Carlo Methods*, SIAM, 1992.
- I. H. Sloan & S. Joe, *Lattice Methods for Multiple Integration*, OUP, 1994.
- A. B. Owen, "Scrambled net variance for integrals of smooth functions," *Ann. Statist.*
  25:1541–1562, 1997; R. Cranley & T. N. L. Patterson (1976) random shifts for lattice error
  estimates.

**Method.** Low-discrepancy point sets give O(N^{-1}(log N)^d) error for bounded-variation
integrands and, with Owen scrambling, O(N^{-3/2+ε}) RMSE for smooth integrands. Indicator functions
of cones are discontinuous so plain QMC gains little — but after the Genz/GHK smoothing (item 1) or
conditional MC (item 8) the integrand is smooth and QMC gains are large.

**Gaps.** (4).

**Application.** If any sampling-like quadrature is acceptable under "deductive", the strongest
practical estimator is: integrate radially/conditionally analytically, then a randomized lattice
(Korobov) rule on the remaining smooth integrand. Even for the raw indicator, sampling on S^9 with
a **spherical t-design / antithetic set** (±x, all coordinate permutations of x — the network's
target is permutation-equivariant, so orbit-averaging is a free exact control) removes much
variance. Antithetic ±x is automatic: the network is homogeneous, so x and cx agree for c>0;
use x and −x as distinct points.

### 8. Conditional Monte Carlo / Rao–Blackwellization along rays and 1-D slices (Hammersley 1956; Trotter & Tukey 1956; Deák 1980)
**Citations.**
- J. M. Hammersley, "Conditional Monte Carlo," *J. ACM* 3:73–76, 1956.
- H. F. Trotter & J. W. Tukey, "Conditional Monte Carlo for normal samples," in *Symposium on
  Monte Carlo Methods* (Meyer, ed.), Wiley, 1956.
- I. Deák, "Three digit accurate multiple normal probabilities," *Numer. Math.* 35:369–380, 1980
  (spherical-radial decomposition: sample directions, integrate radius exactly).

**Method.** Integrate out one or more variables analytically conditional on the rest. Deák's
method samples directions on the sphere and computes the radial integral in closed form via the
chi distribution.

**Gaps.** (4), (2).

**Application.** Radial integration is trivial here (homogeneity ⇒ indicator constant along rays),
so instead condition on a *line*: pick a random point and a direction d (e.g. d = e_{(1)} − e_{(2)},
the tie direction); along x + t d the network is piecewise linear in t with finitely many breakpoints
found exactly by walking the activation pattern; the Gaussian measure of the correct set on that
line is a sum of Φ-differences. This turns each sample into an exact 1-D integral across the error
band — precisely where hit-or-miss MC is wasteful. Variance drops roughly by the ratio band
width / typical scale. Iterating over 2-D slices (spacings s_1,s_2) is also feasible.

### 9. Schläfli, Sommerville, McMullen — angle sums and Schläfli differential formula; Santaló's integral geometry
**Citations.**
- L. Schläfli, *Theorie der vielfachen Kontinuität* (1852; publ. 1901).
- D. M. Y. Sommerville, *An Introduction to the Geometry of N Dimensions*, Methuen, 1929.
- P. McMullen, "Non-linear angle-sum relations for polyhedral cones and polytopes," *Math. Proc.
  Camb. Phil. Soc.* 78:247–261, 1975.
- L. A. Santaló, *Integral Geometry and Geometric Probability*, Addison-Wesley, 1976.
- B. Grünbaum, *Convex Polytopes*, 1967 (Ch. 14, angle sums; Gram–Euler relation).

**Method.** Gram/Sommerville relations: Σ_F (−1)^{dim F} β(0,F)γ(F,C) relations between internal and
external angles of a cone; the conic kinematic formula gives the probability that a random rotation
of one cone meets another as a sum of products of intrinsic volumes. Schläfli: d vol_{n-1}(spherical
simplex) = (1/(n−2)) Σ vol_{n−3}(codim-2 face) dθ.

**Gaps.** (1).

**Application.** Gives exact consistency checks (angle sums over a complete fan must equal 1;
Gram relation) to catch bookkeeping errors when summing cone measures over activation patterns.
Inclusion–exclusion on a fan can be rewritten with Gram relations to avoid computing high-dimensional
pieces. Schläfli is the engine for item 3's facet-tilt expansion.

### 10. Cover (1965), Zaslavsky (1975), Winder (1966) — counting regions of hyperplane arrangements
**Citations.**
- T. M. Cover, "Geometrical and statistical properties of systems of linear inequalities with
  applications in pattern recognition," *IEEE Trans. Electronic Computers* EC-14:326–334, 1965.
- T. Zaslavsky, "Facing up to arrangements: face-count formulas for partitions of space by
  hyperplanes," *Mem. Amer. Math. Soc.* 154, 1975.
- R. O. Winder, "Partitions of N-space by hyperplanes," *SIAM J. Appl. Math.* 14:811–818, 1966.
- T. Zaslavsky, "Counting the faces of cut-up spaces," *Bull. AMS* 81:916–918, 1975.

**Method.** Number of regions of a central arrangement of m hyperplanes in R^d in general position is
C(m,d) = 2 Σ_{k<d} C(m−1,k); in general it is |χ(−1)| for the characteristic polynomial of the
intersection lattice.

**Gaps.** (7), (6).

**Application.** Bounds how many activation-pattern cones exist and, more usefully, how many carry
non-negligible measure. Since gates are mostly determined by rank order, the relevant arrangement
is a coarsening of the braid arrangement (x_i = x_j) plus coordinate hyperplanes; counting its
regions (χ of the braid arrangement is t(t−1)…(t−9)) tells you whether exact enumeration of all
cones with item 1 is affordable (it is not for all 10!·2^10 pieces, but symmetry/merging should
collapse it to a manageable set — e.g. condition only on the top-3 identities and the sign pattern).

### 11. Rice (1944–45) and level-crossing / boundary-layer asymptotics
**Citations.**
- S. O. Rice, "Mathematical analysis of random noise," *Bell Syst. Tech. J.* 23:282–332 (1944),
  24:46–156 (1945).
- H. Cramér & M. R. Leadbetter, *Stationary and Related Stochastic Processes*, Wiley, 1967.
- C. M. Bender & S. A. Orszag, *Advanced Mathematical Methods for Scientists and Engineers*,
  McGraw-Hill, 1978 (Laplace, boundary layers, matched asymptotics).
- N. Bleistein & R. A. Handelsman, *Asymptotic Expansions of Integrals*, 1975.

**Method.** Rice: expected number of crossings of a level = ∫ E[|Ẋ| | X=u] p_X(u). The multi-
dimensional (Kac–Rice) version gives the expected (d−1)-dimensional measure of a level set, i.e. a
surface measure, which times a band width is a boundary-layer volume.

**Gaps.** (2).

**Application.** The error measure ≈ ∫_{tie surface} (signed normal displacement of the network's
decision boundary) dγ_{d−1}. The Rice/Kac–Rice formula evaluates such Gaussian surface integrals as
E[|∇(x_(1)−x_(2))| δ(x_(1)−x_(2)) · w(x)] = density-at-tie × conditional expectation of the local
band width w(x). The conditional expectation is over the 8-D tie surface and can itself be expanded
(it is smooth there), which is the controlled version of the current first/second-order
corrections: corrections should be expanded *on the tie surface*, not around a typical interior
point.

### 12. Hahnloser (1998) and piecewise-linear systems (Chua & Kang 1977)
**Citations.**
- R. H. R. Hahnloser, "On the piecewise analysis of networks of linear threshold neurons," *Neural
  Networks* 11:691–697, 1998. (verified)
- L. O. Chua & S. M. Kang, "Section-wise piecewise-linear functions: canonical representation,
  properties, and applications," *Proc. IEEE* 65:915–929, 1977.
- E. D. Sontag, "Nonlinear regulation: the piecewise linear approach," *IEEE TAC* 26:346–358, 1981.

**Method.** Linear-threshold (ReLU) recurrent nets realize a linear map selected by the active set;
analyze each active set's linear dynamics, permitted/forbidden sets, and transitions. Chua–Kang give
canonical (absolute-value) representations of PWL maps, which make region boundaries explicit.

**Gaps.** (6).

**Application.** Supports building the per-pattern linear map exactly: for gate sequence
(D_1,…,D_10), logits = W_o D_10 W_h D_9 … W_i x_t sums, a single 10×10 matrix per pattern. Hahnloser's
"permitted set" idea suggests that the hidden state lives in a few dominant active sets for most
inputs (rank-driven), justifying a tree enumeration of patterns ordered by probability (prune
branches whose cone measure, by item 1, is < tolerance).

### 13. Edgeworth / Gram–Charlier and saddlepoint expansions (Daniels 1954; Barndorff-Nielsen & Cox 1989)
**Citations.**
- H. E. Daniels, "Saddlepoint approximations in statistics," *Ann. Math. Statist.* 25:631–650, 1954.
- R. Lugannani & S. Rice, "Saddle point approximation for the distribution of the sum of
  independent random variables," *Adv. Appl. Probab.* 12:475–490, 1980.
- O. E. Barndorff-Nielsen & D. R. Cox, *Asymptotic Techniques for Use in Statistics*, Chapman & Hall,
  1989.

**Method.** Expand a density around a Gaussian using cumulants (Edgeworth) or tilt to a saddle point
(Daniels; Lugannani–Rice tail formula).

**Gaps.** (5).

**Application.** Lower priority: logit margins are piecewise-linear in x, so their distribution is a
mixture of Gaussians (one per cone) rather than a near-Gaussian sum — Edgeworth is the wrong
shape. Possible use: approximate the distribution of the margin "logit_correct − max other" inside a
dominant cone as Gaussian + skew correction to get P(margin<0) cheaply as a sanity check.

## Suggested pipeline synthesizing the above
1. Reduce by symmetry: accuracy = Σ over (identity of top-3 indices, sign pattern of
   inputs) — or better, condition only on what actually changes gates.
2. For each dominant activation pattern, form the cone, prune redundant constraints by LP, compute
   its Gaussian measure with Genz/GHK + randomized lattice rule (items 1, 7), or 1-D factor integrals
   when the cone is Weyl-chamber-like (item 5).
3. For the thin tie bands, compute leading-order error analytically with tube/Schläfli/Rice
   (items 2, 3, 11) using spacing densities (item 6), and use those as **control variates** for
   any residual numerical integration.
4. Use angle-sum identities (item 9) and region counts (item 10) as bookkeeping checks.
